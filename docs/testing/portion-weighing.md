# Portion Weighing Sheet

**What this is for:** it gives the ground truth for STEADY's accuracy test (`dataset-v5`) and for the portion table, which turns "1 katori" into grams. Real weights from a real kitchen beat any number found online. Online sources disagree: a roti is listed at anywhere from 30 g to 50 g.

**You need:** a kitchen scale (1 g precision is ideal), your usual plates, bowls and katoris, and about 2 minutes per item.

## How to weigh
1. Put the **empty** plate, bowl or katori on the scale and press **tare** so it reads 0.
2. Serve the food exactly as you normally would. Don't make it neater or fuller than usual.
3. Write down the grams.
4. **For dishes, write down how much oil or ghee went into the whole pot,** and how many servings it made. Oil is the biggest hidden calorie source.
5. If you can, weigh the same thing on **2–3 different days**. That shows how much a "normal" portion varies.

## Also useful (if you have time)
- **Your katori and bowl sizes:** fill each with water and weigh it. That's its capacity in ml.
- **One full home meal, weighed component by component:** for example roti + dal + sabzi + rice on one plate.
- **2–3 restaurant or delivery meals** where the menu or app shows official calories. Note the source.
