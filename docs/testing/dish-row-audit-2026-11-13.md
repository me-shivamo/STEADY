# Indian dish-row audit, 2026-11-13

Read-only audit (no database or repo writes) of the whole-dish rows the resolver used in the split-vs-whole A/B (dataset-v3 dev set). Values come from evidence (lab-measured dishes, INDB/IFCT recipe maths, published studies, `docs/research/reports/Indian portion sizes and cooking yields.md`), never from eval bands. dataset-v4 was not used. One finder, one evidence researcher per suspect, and one adversarial reviewer. **Nothing here is applied yet.** Founder decisions are needed where marked.

## Suspects found

### biryani / chicken biryani (`biryani_chicken`, row_value, now 104 kcal/100 g)

- **Source:** usda (FNDDS 2706538 'Biryani with chicken')
- **How it was used:** v01 'ate some biryani': whole variants hit the exact key 'biryani' in 6/6 reps (350-400 g = 364-416 kcal). i15 'a plate of chicken biryani': no row in any whole rep, so AI estimate 150 (mini) / 180 (luna) x 400 g. Split variants broke it into rice 130 + AI chicken + AI sauce.
- **Problem:** Two problems. (1) The row is an American FNDDS recipe. It uses 668 g baked potato against 632 g cooked rice, 576 g skinless chicken and only 28 g butter in about 2.97 kg, which gives 76% water and 2.4 g fat. That is not an Indian biryani. Recipe maths on INDB ASC122 mutton biryani (100 g rice, 100 g mutton, 16 g oil), with the rice taken to the report's 3.0x yield, gives about 129 kcal/100 g (F 5.2). If the fat is read as the 4 tbsp the research notes say INDB meant, it gives about 174 (F 10.7). FNDDS's own 'Biryani with meat' is 145 (F 6.8). So 104 is 20-40% low; an evidence range is about 130-170 for chicken (P about 7, F about 5-7). (2) The phrase 'chicken biryani', the most-ordered dish in India, has no row and falls to an AI estimate. Its 150-180 is in fact closer to the evidence than the stored row.

### poha (`poha_raw_weight`, row_value, now 294.5 kcal/100 g)

- **Source:** indb (BFP044, published raw weight)
- **How it was used:** i13 'a plate of poha': exact key hit in 9/12 reps. mini-split and mini-whole used 350 g = 1030 kcal; luna-whole used 200-300 g = 589-884 kcal. luna-split decomposed it (AI cooked poha 130-170).
- **Problem:** INDB's recipe has no soak water: 40 g flakes + 30 g potato + 10 g peanut + 10 g oil = 109 g for 2 bowls, 161 kcal a bowl. The research report names this exact error ('Its poha reads 294 kcal because the soak water is missing'). NIN's 270 kcal per 200 ml cup holds about 50 g of dry poha, which implies home poha at about 150-200 kcal/100 g. The Pune lab's 349 (22.3% water) is flagged by the report as an unusually dry sample. INDB's own wetter variants agree: BFP045 vegetable poha 180.5 (P4.9 C21.5 F8.1) and ASC484 poha with curd 173. The row is about 1.6-1.9x too dense. The parser's 200-350 g plate makes it worse (the report's plate is 150 g). 053 deferred poha pending a cooking test; an interim of about 175-180 (BFP045) is evidence-backed.

### pav bhaji (`pav_bhaji_plate`, portion, now 96.53 kcal/100 g)

- **Source:** indb (OSR112, published raw weight)
- **How it was used:** i09 'a plate of pav bhaji': whole variants used the exact key in 6/6 reps (mini 400 g = 386 kcal; luna 300-350 g = 290-338). The split variants (pav + bhaji, AI) gave 538-1000.
- **Problem:** The hypothesis that this row is 'bhaji without pav' is wrong. OSR112 includes 516 g of white buns (18% of the 2.9 kg mix, about 2.5 pav a serving) and 121.5 g butter, diluted by 972 g of added water kept with nothing evaporated. The per-100 g is also within 9% of the Pune lab's measured pav bhaji (106 kcal, 76.2% water, 'bread with mix vegetables'), and that sample has the same pav share (about 18%, from its water balance). So per-100 g evidence does not justify raising it. The gap is per plate. INDB's own serving is 581 g raw / 561 kcal; at lab density the same serving weighs 529 g. The parser's 300-400 g 'plate' therefore logs about 1.3 pav worth (290-386 kcal). Fix with a '1 plate' portion tied to 2 pav + bhaji (about 450-550 g at this density, about 480-560 kcal), or log pav count x pav row + bhaji. Lower priority than the rest of this list.

### pizza_indb_raw: fix_match (confidence A)

- **Current:** Exact key 'pizza' goes to INDB BFP122 'Pizza' at 146.8 kcal, P 6.85, C 9.94, F 8.72, fibre 1.71 per 100 g, by published raw weight. I rebuilt the recipe line by line with scripts/recalc/indb.py and it matches to 0.01. It is a home mushroom-and-sausage pizza with very little base and a lot of topping: 594.6 g raw in total, of which 60 g maida (10.1%), 130 g raw pork sausage (UK CoFID), 100 g mushroom, 125 g tomato, 60 g cabbage, capsicum and onion stalk, 20 g onion, 50 g mozzarella, 13 g oil, 30 ml water and 3 g yeast extract. No baking loss is applied. Its energy split is P 19% / C 27% / F 54%, while real pizza is about 18 / 50 / 33. 053 did not touch this row.

Even a cooking yield cannot rescue it. Recipe totals are 873 kcal, C 59.1 g, P 40.7 g, F 51.8 g. Raw water is about 417 g (70%): 287 g from the IFCT lines, 30 g added water, sausage at about 57% and mozzarella at about 50%. A 10-20% bake loss gives 163-183 kcal and C 11.0-12.4 g per 100 g. Even forcing it down to the 43% water of measured chain pizza (a 53% yield, which is not realistic) gives 279 kcal but only 18.9 g carbs. The trouble is the recipe's make-up, not its yield. The row is correct for its own recipe and wrong for the bare word 'pizza'.

In the DEV runs, case v05 hit this row in 5 of 12 reps: 150 g came to 220 kcal, and 280-300 g came to 411-440 kcal.

## Cross-cutting issues

- The generator is hard-wired to 053. recalc.py writes supabase/migrations/053_recalc_indian_v1.sql, derives ids as UUIDv5(recalc_v1|variant|key), and its `check` diffs against the committed 053 files. Every proposal that says 'add to targets.csv / custom_recipes.json', and especially 'change upma_rava', would regenerate or alter the live, run-once 053. Batch 2 needs its own mode (new migration number, new variant keys or calc_version, a fresh snapshot.json covering every touched row: a6958211, the BFP044 poha row, BFP122, 137f6250, 7653a3be, cd15763a, 1abbe3e2) and must never edit an existing 053 variant.
- The engine cannot do what several proposals assume. recipe_custom always uses keep (Wc = W), with no target moisture and no w_override, and lab: anchors resolve only for INDB codes. Target-moisture custom recipes (biryani 65%, upma 70%) need either an explicit, documented K505 water line or a tested engine change. External lab anchors (Khan 2019, Mili 2024, Korrapati 2018) belong in a separate external-anchors file. They must not go into lab.csv, which drives calibrate, the params fit and the frozen split.
- Energy convention: every new row must store E = 4P + 9F + 4C_avl + 2·fibre (053 check |kcal - E| <= 0.5). The poha proposal stores INDB's summed energy (190.07 instead of 193.1); recheck any value copied from an INDB-convention script.
- fts (live) reads exact keys only; aliases serve in ranked mode only (macroResolver.ts lines 733-743), and normalizeName neither stems nor folds spellings. Alias-based fixes (pizza 2a/2b, all the biryani/upma/rajma aliases) do nothing in production. The copy-row workaround multiplies identical verified rows (aloo x5, bhindi x4, rajma x2, upma x2, biryani x2, pizza). Consider one resolver change instead, letting fts read curated verified-only aliases at the same precedence as in ranked mode (they already outrank machine memory). Short of that, every copy row needs snapshot-guard and search-check entries.
- Every new exact key is a founder content decision (053 approved 'chole', 'phulka' and 'chawal' individually). The proposals self-approve keys 'by pattern' (rajma 'decision 2/5 pattern', aloo 'ship now'). recalc.py requires approved_by on key_copy, so collect real approvals.
- Portions: 054 is run-once and keyed by 053 ids. New rows and copy rows need portion_category_map entries and founder portion_units rows, or table mode falls back to word or category rows. copy_reference carries the source fdc_id, so 054's fdc-keyed USDA units follow it (pizza '1 pizza' = 897 g via fdc 173291). The 150 g katori founder decision covers the dal and legume family only; sabzi uses 054's 110 g (bhindi) and 120 g (dry sabzi) katoris.
- Density and plate weight are coupled. The 054 poha_upma plate of 150 g was built from 40-50 g of dry grain. Making poha and upma wetter (60.6% / 70-72%) while keeping 150 g plates cuts the implied grain to 31-35 g and overstates the per-plate drop. Re-derive grain-dish plates from dry grain (NIN 50 g per cup) whenever the density changes.
- The oil default is applied inconsistently across the proposals. Upma overrides INDB oil on Pune fat-in-dry-matter evidence. Poha keeps INDB oil despite the same evidence (Pune 11.7% of dry matter against INDB 23.2%, and NIN 408 vs 270 kcal per 50 g cup). Bhindi overrides on Pune and Leeds but ignores Khan 2019's Peshawar okra at 11.6 g fat, which supports the current row. Give the founder one oil-policy question (INDB as written, a home-lean option, regional variants) with the full measured range per dish, instead of dish-by-dish overrides.
