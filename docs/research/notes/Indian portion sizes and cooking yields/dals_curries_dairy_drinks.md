# Indian dals, curries, dairy and drinks: measured cooking yields, water content, household servings and oil per serving

Scope: dal/dal tadka, rajma, chole, dry sabzi (aloo, bhindi), paneer gravy, curd, chai, a glass of milk, chaas, and oats with milk. Research date 2026-11-13.

Method notes for the report writer:
- Four primary documents were downloaded and decoded locally, so the numbers quoted from them are verbatim table values, not search snippets:
  - Chiplonkar & Agte 2007 (Appendix I);
  - NIN *Dietary Guidelines for Indians* (DGI) 2011 (Annexures 2, 2a, 8 and the measures page);
  - NIN DGI 2024 (sample meal plans and complementary-feeding annexures);
  - the INDB `recipes.xlsx` + `recipes_servingsize.xlsx` files.
- Items marked "(search snippet)" were not read in full.
- "Inference" means arithmetic or reasoning by this researcher on the cited numbers.

## 1. What do ICMR-NIN publications give (DGI 2024, DGI 2011, household measures, per-serving values)?

### Takeaway
NIN gives official household-measure definitions:
- DGI 2011: cup = 200 ml, tsp = 5 ml, tbsp = 15 ml.
- DGI 2024: katoris of 115/150 ml (small), 200 ml (medium) and 360 ml (big); 1 tsp oil/ghee = 5 g.
- Per-meal raw amounts: about 20–30 g pulses per meal, 100 ml curd, 5–15 g oil.
- DGI 2011 also gives per-serving energy for cooked dishes ("plain dhal ½ cup = 100 kcal", "tea with 2 tsp sugar + 50 ml toned milk = 75 kcal").

These are planning standards, not measured intakes. NIN does not publish cooked-dal moisture or dry→cooked yields.

### Inferences
- NIN dal, worked out:
  - "½ cup dhal" = **100 ml**, because NIN's cup is 200 ml, not 240 ml.
  - It is built from **20 g raw pulse**, so it is a thick dal: ~20 g dry pulse per ~100 g, roughly 75–78% water.
  - It carries ~3–4 g fat to reach 100 kcal (20 g pulse ≈ 65 kcal + ~35 kcal fat).
- NIN tea, worked out:
  - 75 kcal per cup ≈ 50 ml toned milk (~29 kcal) + 2 tsp sugar (~8–10 g, ~32–40 kcal).
  - In the 2024 plans the "evening beverage" is 100 ml milk = 70 kcal before sugar.
- NIN's 115–360 ml katori ladder is the only official katori volume set found. The 150 ml and 200 ml sizes are the natural "small" and "medium" defaults for an app.
- Implied oil per cooked dish: NIN puts 10–15 g oil into a whole lunch (dal + vegetable curry + GLV curry), which is ~3–7 g per dish.

### Gaps
- The DGI 2024 Annexure I/II tables (katori/cup/spoon pictures and "raw food item measures using household utensils") could not be decoded. It was not confirmed whether DGI 2024 keeps the 2011 cooked-preparation kcal annexure.
- The NIN "Nutritive Value of Indian Foods" household-measure tables and NIN exchange lists were not retrieved.
- NIN gives no cooked-dal moisture, no dry→cooked yield, and no tadka-fat figures.

## 2. What do lab studies give for moisture of home-cooked Indian dals and legume curries, and how does it vary by region and thickness?

### Takeaway
The one robust Indian lab dataset is Chiplonkar & Agte 2007 (Pune).
- Its pressure-cooked dals and whole-legume curries are **83–89% water** at **50–86 kcal/100 g**, with **1.7–3.6 g fat/100 g**.
- A thick lentil preparation is 68.6% water and 142 kcal.
- Dry vegetable preparations are 68–81% water, with 133 kcal and 3.6 g fat (potato) and 156 kcal and 5.9 g fat (ladies finger).
- Sweet milky tea is 86.2% water at 63 kcal/100 g.

No measured regional (North vs South) moisture comparison was found.

### Cited Findings
**Study design.** Chiplonkar & Agte, Agharkar Research Institute, Pune (*Asia Pac J Clin Nutr* 2007;16(2):227–239).
- Scope: 104 individual cooked foods and 92 composite meals.
- Sourcing: "Grains were brought from open market where material comes from different parts of India."
- Legumes: "split red gram, split Bengal gram, split green gram, split black gram, whole green gram, lentil, moth beans, field beans, cow pea, green peas, soyabean, black beans, dried French beans and whole horse gram were pressure cooked."
- Vegetables were "cooked in pans using oil and adding traditional spices, salt and jaggery".
- Meals were "prepared in the most traditional manner in each region".
- Each sample was homogenised and analysed in replicates (prior STEADY notes record 5 independent replicates in duplicate) using NIN manual methods.
- Source: [APJCN 2007](https://apjcn.qdu.edu.cn/16_2_22.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Implied raw pulse per 100 g cooked.** Computed from cooked protein ÷ raw-pulse protein. IFCT raw dal protein is ~21–24 g/100 g; this is an approximate inference.
  - Pune toor dal curry: 2.5 ÷ ~21.7 ≈ **11–12 g raw dal per 100 g**.
  - Green gram: ≈ 10–11 g.
  - Whole Bengal gram curry: 1.9 ÷ ~19 ≈ 10 g.
  - Lentil (thick): 6.5 ÷ ~25 ≈ 26 g.
  
  So the Pune home-style dals and chana curries are **thin-to-medium (~10–12 g dry legume/100 g)**, and the lentil row is thick (~26 g/100 g).
- The Pune toor dal's CHO (10.7 g) exceeds what ~11.5 g of dal supplies (~6.5 g). The excess (~4 g) is consistent with Maharashtrian *amti* made with **jaggery and tamarind**, which the paper says were added for taste.
- Regional bias: Pune (West) amti-style dal is thin and sweet-sour. North Indian dal tadka and dal makhani and Punjabi chole are usually thicker and richer (see Q3 recipes), and South Indian sambar includes vegetables and tamarind. No study measured these regional differences.
- Thickness bands suggested by the evidence:

  | Band | Water | Dry pulse | Energy | Evidence |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Thin | ~87–89% | ~10 g/100 g | ~50 kcal | INDB thin dals; Pune split bengal gram 89% |
  | Medium | ~83–85% | ~12–15 g | ~65–80 kcal | Pune toor/moong |
  | Thick | ~70–78% | ~20–26 g | ~95–145 kcal | NIN ½ cup dal; Pune lentil 68.6%; USDA dal 66.3% |

- The vegetable rows imply **home dry-sabzi fat of ~3.6–6 g/100 g**, i.e. ~4–9 g per 120–150 g serving. That is lower than the INDB recipes in Q3.

### Gaps
- Not obtained:
  - Indian lab moisture data for **rajma curry**, Punjabi **chole**, **dal makhani**, **paneer butter masala** or **palak paneer**;
  - any North vs South vs West comparison of the same dish;
  - restaurant versions of these dishes.
- Pune energy for vegetables looks high relative to fat (CHO is probably "by difference", which includes fibre). Treat vegetable kcal as an upper bound.
- Other Indian proximate studies of cooked dishes (e.g. a Himachal legume-food study (search snippet only); [ARCC DR-2099](https://arccjournals.com/journal/asian-journal-of-dairy-and-food-research/DR-2099)) were not read.

## 3. What do standardized-recipe and portion-size studies in India report (raw ingredient weights, cooked yield, katori sizes, oil per serving)?

### Takeaway
INDB's standardized recipes define one "bowl" serving of dal as **30 g dry dal + 180–300 ml water + 5–10 g oil/ghee**, with 5 g ghee per tadka.
- Legume curries: 30–40 g dry legume + 7.5–10 g oil.
- Dry sabzi: 100–120 g vegetable + 5–15 g oil.
- Paneer gravies: 40–75 g paneer + 7.5–37.5 g fat.
- Raita: 100 g curd.
- Oats: 20 g oats + 250 ml milk.

INDB applies no yield factor, so its "bowl" weights are raw-ingredient totals.

Indian utensil surveys find bowls far larger than NIN's 200 ml cup (240/320/600 ml in Haryana). Population visible-fat intake is 16–46 g/day, with the North highest.

### Inferences
- **INDB bowls are pre-evaporation totals**, so they overstate served weight for dals and understate it for dry-basis curries (rajma/chole with no water).
  - A washed moong dal of 282 g raw total, simmered and served, is probably ~200–250 g. That is consistent with ~10–14 g dry dal/100 g served, which agrees with the Pune lab dals.
- **Tadka/oil per dal serving:**
  - INDB home dals: 5–10 g.
  - Pune lab toor dal: 1.7 g/100 g → ~2.6 g per 150 g.
  - NIN ½-cup dal: ~3–4 g.
  - INDB restaurant-style dal makhani: ~19 g; moti mahal dal: ~20 g.
  
  **Home ≈ 3–5 g; restaurant/"makhani" ≈ 15–20 g** per bowl, a 3–5× spread.
- **Oil per dry sabzi serving:** INDB 5–15 g (median ~10 g) vs Pune lab 3.6–5.9 g fat/100 g (≈4–9 g per 120–150 g). INDB recipe oil is probably at the high end of home practice.
- **Paneer gravy fat** spans 7.5 g (palak paneer, home) to ~38 g of fat ingredients (shahi paneer). Restaurant "butter masala" styles fall at the top of that range, but no Indian lab measurement was found.
- Utensil-survey bowls (240–600 ml) are much bigger than NIN's 150–200 ml katoris. Portion error, not composition, is likely the larger source of error for dals and curries.

### Gaps
- No Indian lab study was found that measures **oil per serving in restaurant dal tadka, chole, rajma or paneer butter masala**. The safefood (Ireland) "What's in your Indian takeaway?" lab survey and the Ashwini et al. full text both returned 403.
- No measured study was found of **household tadka ghee per serving**.
- The INDDEX24 / FAO-WHO GIFT India, CARRS and PURE portion-size methods were not retrieved.
- The Lady Irwin College photo atlas and the MDRF/Dr Mohan's "Atlas of Indian Foods" portion weights were not accessed.

## 4. What do USDA cooking-yield/retention tables and Bognár (2002) give for boiled legumes (dry → cooked)?

### Takeaway
No primary USDA or Bognár legume yield table could be retrieved:
- USDA AH-102 is a scanned PDF with no text layer;
- the FDC API was rate-limited;
- Bognár's tables were not found online.

The best accessible evidence puts **boiled, drained legumes at ~2.2–3.1× dry weight**:
- lentils 3.12× (Brazilian measured study, snippet);
- chickpeas 2.2–2.5× (commercial blogs, low confidence);
- USDA's boiled-lentil-based "Dal" implies ~3×.

Finished Indian dals and curries are far higher, at **~5–10× dry weight**, because of the gravy water.

### Cited Findings
- USDA Agriculture Handbook No. 102, "Food Yields Summarized by Different Stages of Preparation" (Matthews & Garrison 1975 revision), is the classic USDA source for dry-bean cooked yields. — [EPA HERO record](https://heronext.epa.gov/reference/1065456)
  - The PDF was downloaded from [ARS](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/80400525/Data/Classics/ah102.pdf) but is image-only, so no values could be extracted.
- A Brazilian study of weight-change indicators reports a moist-heat cooking "conversion index" of **1.05 for green beans up to 3.12 for lentils** (search snippet; the full text failed to load twice). — [DEMETRA (UERJ)](https://www.e-publicacoes.uerj.br/demetra/article/download/84700/53342/337395)
- USDA FNDDS "Dal" = boiled lentils 96 g + ghee 4 g; water 66.3 g/100 g; 145 kcal. — [FDC 2707427](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2707427/nutrients)
- Chickpeas, commercial-blog figures (low confidence):
  - "100 grams of dry chickpeas will yield approximately 240 to 300 grams";
  - another blog gives "220g to 250g";
  - "2.2 to 2.5 times".
  
  Source: [Country Life Foods blog](https://countrylifefoods.com/blogs/beans-and-legumes/calculating-chickpeas-dry-to-cooked-weight)
- The EuroFIR recipe guideline defines yield factor = total cooked weight / total raw ingredient weight. It recommends a cooking test or literature values such as Bognár 2002 (prior STEADY notes). — [EuroFIR guideline PDF](https://www.eurofir.org/wp-admin/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/EUROFIR-RECIPE-GUIDELINE_FINAL.pdf)

### Inferences
- Moisture-based yield. Yield ≈ (100 − raw water%) ÷ (100 − cooked water%), assuming no solids loss. Raw Indian dals are ~9–12% water (IFCT).
  - **Pune toor dal curry at 84.9% water** → (100−10)/(100−84.9) × (dal share of dry matter, ~0.8) ≈ **~5–6× dry dal weight as served**.
  - **Thin INDB-style dal (~87%)** → ~7–9×.
  - **Thick NIN-style ½-cup dal (~76%)** → ~4–5×.
  - **USDA-style thick dal (66%)** → ~3×.
- Pressure-cooked plain legume before tempering: by analogy with the lentil index (3.12) and the chickpea blogs (2.2–2.5), a reasonable working value is **~2.3–2.6× for whole legumes (rajma, chole) and ~2.8–3.2× for split dals**. These are unverified against USDA/Bognár primary data.
- For a calorie app, store dal and legume curries as **"g dry legume + g fat per serving"** and treat yield as a function of chosen thickness. Do not rely on a single dry→cooked factor.

### Gaps
- Not obtained:
  - USDA SR Legacy raw vs boiled water values for lentils, chickpeas, kidney beans, mung beans, pigeon peas and split peas (FDC DEMO_KEY rate-limited);
  - AH-102 legume rows (scanned PDF);
  - Bognár 2002 legume yield factors;
  - FAO/INFOODS yield values.
  
  These would let the moisture-based yields above be checked. A follow-up with an FDC API key or an OCR'd AH-102 is recommended.
- No Indian measured study of pressure-cooker dal water ratios or yields was retrieved. An ICRISAT pigeonpea dhal cooking-quality PDF returned 401.

## 5. Where sources disagree (e.g. NIN "½ cup dal = 100 kcal" vs lab dals ~68 kcal/100 g), why?

### Takeaway
Almost all of the disagreement is **water (dal thickness) plus added fat**, with a minor contribution from cup-size definitions. Pulse composition plays almost no part. The sources describe different dals:
- INDB: thin, all cooking water counted, ~50 kcal/100 g;
- Pune lab: medium-thin, ~68 kcal/100 g;
- NIN: thick ½ cup = ~100 kcal per ~100 g;
- USDA: thick lentils + 4% ghee, 145 kcal/100 g.

On a "g dry pulse + g fat" basis, they converge.

### Cited Findings
- NIN: "Plain dhal ½ cup 100 kcal", cup = 200 ml, built from "Pulses 20 g → Dhal ½ Cup". — [NIN DGI 2011](https://nin.res.in/downloads/DietaryGuidelinesforNINwebsite.pdf)
- Pune lab: split redgram curry 84.9% water, 68 kcal, 1.7 g fat/100 g. — [APJCN 2007](https://apjcn.qdu.edu.cn/16_2_22.pdf)
- INDB: washed moong dal = 30 g dal + 240 ml water + 5 g oil (no evaporation); INDB lists it at 50.0 kcal/100 g (prior STEADY notes). — [recipes.xlsx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lindsayjaacks/Indian-Nutrient-Databank-INDB-/main/recipes.xlsx)
- USDA FNDDS Dal: 66.3% water, 145 kcal (boiled lentils + 4% ghee). — [FDC 2707427](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2707427/nutrients)
- Chai has the same pattern:
  - NIN: 1 cup tea (50 ml toned milk + 2 tsp sugar) = 75 kcal;
  - Pune lab: tea 63 kcal/100 g;
  - INDB: 25 ml milk + 1 tsp sugar per cup, with all 180 ml of water counted (INDB lists 16.14 kcal/100 g in STEADY's local INDB copy, `scripts/data/indb.json`, code ASC001).
  
  Sources: [NIN DGI 2011](https://nin.res.in/downloads/DietaryGuidelinesforNINwebsite.pdf); [APJCN 2007](https://apjcn.qdu.edu.cn/16_2_22.pdf); [recipes.xlsx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lindsayjaacks/Indian-Nutrient-Databank-INDB-/main/recipes.xlsx)

### Inferences
- **NIN dal vs Pune lab dal:**
  - NIN ½ cup = 100 ml ≈ 100–105 g, holding **20 g raw pulse + ~3–4 g fat** → ~95–100 kcal/100 g.
  - The Pune dal holds **~11–12 g raw pulse + 1.7 g fat** per 100 g → 68 kcal.
  
  The ~1.45× gap is about 70% thickness (pulse concentration 20 vs ~12 g) and about 30% tadka fat (3.5 vs 1.7 g). It is not a composition disagreement.
- **Cup definitions matter:**
  - NIN cup = 200 ml;
  - INDB and USDA cup = 240 ml;
  - Pune "1 cup milk" = 125 g;
  - NIN's coffee cup = 150 ml.
  
  An app that maps "1 cup dal" to 240 g (USDA) would overstate NIN's serving by 20% before any thickness effect.
- **Per-serving totals converge:** INDB's bowl (30 g dal + 5 g oil ≈ 141 kcal), NIN's two ½-cup servings (200 ml ≈ 200 kcal from 40 g pulse) and a Pune 150 g katori (~100 kcal) differ mainly in dry pulse per serving (17–40 g).
- **Chai convergence:** Pune tea at 63 kcal/100 g × a ~120 g cup ≈ **76 kcal**, which matches NIN's **75 kcal/cup**. Two independent sources agree. INDB's chai is a lighter recipe (~35 kcal/cup).
- **STEADY-specific flag:** STEADY's internal review shows the current chai serving as "1 cup = 240 g" at 49 kcal/100 g (118 kcal) (`/home/shivam/STEADY/docs/reviews/053-recalc-indian-v1.md`). Both NIN (200 ml cup, 75 kcal) and the Pune lab (≈76 kcal per 120 g) point to a smaller Indian teacup. The 240 g cup is probably 1.5–2× too large.

### Gaps
- No source gives regional dal thickness distributions, i.e. what share of households make thin vs thick dal. That would be needed to set an evidence-based "medium" default by region.

## 6. Recommended defaults per item (grams per serving S/M/L, dry→cooked yield, water content, oil per serving, confidence)

### Takeaway
Use NIN katori volumes (150 ml small, 200 ml medium, 360 ml big) as the serving ladder. Model dals and legume curries as dry legume + fat with a thickness setting, and keep oil as an explicit adjustable component with separate home and restaurant values.

Confidence is graded as follows:
- **A:** multiple measured sources agree.
- **B:** one good measured or official source.
- **C:** weak or secondary.

- **Thickness selector:** for dals, offer thin / medium / thick. Map the settings to ~50 / ~70 / ~100–140 kcal per 100 g before tadka, using 10 / 12–13 / 20–26 g dry pulse per 100 g. Then add tadka fat separately: home 4–5 g, restaurant 10–20 g.

- **Measured sources not found:**

- Paneer gravy moisture and fat for home and restaurant versions are unmeasured. The paneer row is the weakest in the table.

- Unverified dry→boiled legume yields: USDA SR moisture-based values, AH-102 and Bognár 2002 remain gaps.
