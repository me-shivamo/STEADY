# Retention Science & "Close the Ring" Psychology for Nutrition Tracking

*Compiled 2026-10-27 for the STEADY redesign (lean-professional gainer persona, Apple-style rings, goal-aware feedback).*

*Evidence labels used throughout:*
- ***[PEER]*** *= peer-reviewed paper*
- ***[INDUSTRY]*** *= company or first-party data, or product documentation*
- ***[OPINION]*** *= commentary, blogs, coaches, press, forums*
- ***[WEAK]*** *= small sample, cross-sectional, self-report, pilot, or unverified secondary summary*

*Older findings carry their year. Where a figure came only from a search-engine summary of a page rather than the page itself, it is flagged "verify".*

## 1. Why and when do people quit calorie/food trackers, and what single daily behaviour best predicts success?

### Takeaway
Drop-off is brutal and early:
- **Helander (2014):** only 2.58% of about 190k photo food-loggers became "active".
- **Laing (2014 RCT):** MyFitnessPal use fell from 97% of patients in month 1 to 35% in month 6, and patients called it "too tedious".
- **First 7 days:** across MyFitnessPal, Lose It and Duolingo data, behaviour in the first week predicts the long run.

The behaviour most consistently linked to success is **logging frequency and consistency** (days logged, times per day). Precision and hitting the calorie number on a given day show no such link.

### Cited Findings
- **[PEER] (2014)** Helander, Kaipainen, Korhonen & Wansink, JMIR. Among 189,770 people who used the free photo food-logging app "The Eatery" at least once (Oct 2011 to Apr 2012), only **2.58% (4,895)** became "Actives" (10 or more photos and at least 1 week of use). "Dropouts" posted 0–1 photos. — [JMIR 2014;16(4):e109](https://jmir.org/2014/4/e109); [PMC4004142](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4004142)
- **[PEER] (2014)** Laing et al., Annals of Internal Medicine (doi 10.7326/M13-3005). **212 primary-care patients** with BMI ≥25 got usual care with or without help installing MyFitnessPal for 6 months.
  - **Usage:** app open rates fell from **97% in month 1 to 35% in month 6**.
  - **Weight:** no significant difference (app −0.03 kg vs control +0.3 kg).
  - **Why:** Laing said "Many patients felt it was too tedious, but some patients enjoy it and like getting the feedback on their daily caloric intake."
  - [MDedge](https://www.mdedge.com/content/most-weight-loss-apps-are-losers); [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/six-month-trial-finds-calorie-tracking-app-too-time-intensive)
- **[PEER] (2019)** Gordon, Althoff & Leskovec (WWW '19) analysed **1.4 million MyFitnessPal users** with weight-loss goals.
  - **Day-7 prediction:** first-week behaviour predicted eventual goal achievement with **79% ROC AUC**.
  - **Predictors:** early food intake, motivation to self-monitor, and attitude toward the goal.
  - **Early loss:** fast early weight loss was associated with *higher* achievement.
  - [arXiv 1904.02813](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1904.02813); [PMC7197296](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7197296)
- **[PEER] (2016)** Serrano et al. mined Lose It! data (about 12.4M users; 972,687-user subsample; CART analysis).
  - **Overall:** about **15%** reached 5% weight loss.
  - **By engagement group:** "occasional users" **4.87%**, "basic users" **37.61%**, "power users" **72.70%**.
  - Groups were defined by behavioural and app-engagement variables, and they replicated in holdout samples.
  - [JMIR 2016;18(6):e154 / PMC4925935](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4925935/); [PSU Pure](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/mining-health-app-data-to-find-more-and-less-successful-weight-lo/)
- **[PEER] (2019)** Harvey et al., "Log Often, Lose More" (Obesity). 142 participants (90.8% female, mean BMI 35.8) in a 24-week online program.
  - **Time:** logging took **23.2 min/day in month 1 and 14.6 min/day in month 6**. Only **65.5%** were still logging at month 6.
  - **Time didn't matter:** minutes spent logging did not differ by success.
  - **Frequency did:** ≥5% losers logged in **2.4 vs 1.6 times/day**, and ≥10% losers **2.7 vs 1.7 times/day** (both P<0.001).
  - [PubMed 30801989](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30801989/)
  - The "three or more times per day, consistent day after day" quote is press framing, not in the abstract. — [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/324562)
- **[PEER] (2019)** Patel et al. RCT (n=105, 12 weeks, MyFitnessPal). Median diet-tracking days per week:
  - **5.3 (IQR 1.8–6.7)** when diet and weight were tracked together from day 1
  - **2.9** with diet-only tracking
  - **1.9** when diet tracking was added after week 4
  - The differences were not statistically significant. — [JMIR mHealth 2019;7(2):e12209](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2019/2/e12209/PDF)
- **[PEER] (2015, HCI)** Cordeiro et al., "Barriers and Negative Nudges" (CHI 2015). A survey of 141 current and lapsed food journalers plus 5,526 forum posts across 3 apps found three themes:
  1. **Barriers to reliable food entry** (effort, database gaps, portions)
  2. **"Negative nudges"** created by the journal itself
  3. Problems with social features
  - [Paper PDF](https://my.eng.utah.edu/~cs5540/au16/readings/PersonalInformatics-Cordeiro2015.pdf); [UW CSE news](https://news.cs.washington.edu/2015/04/16/barriers-and-negative-nudges-uw-cse-research-team-led-by-james-fogarty-co-authors-new-study-of-mobile-food-journaling/)
- **[INDUSTRY]** Duolingo:
  - Retention is most fragile in **"the first seven days"** (retention lead, 600+ streak experiments). — [Lenny's Newsletter, Dec 2024](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks)
  - Learners who reach a 7-day streak are **2.4× more likely to come back the next day**. — [Duolingo blog: Improving the streak](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)
- **[INDUSTRY, WEAK]** Cross-category mobile benchmarks:
  - Adjust medians: about **26% D1, 13% D7, 7% D30**. Health & fitness D1 is about **27%**.
  - AppsFlyer 2025 reportedly says the average app loses more than 70% of users within 30 days.
  - Category-specific D7/D30 figures were not verified. — [Adjust](https://www.adjust.com/blog/what-makes-a-good-retention-rate/); [Braze](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/mobile-app-retention)

### Inferences
- **When people quit:** the data points to the **first 7 days**, not "day 3" or "week 2" specifically. Evidence: Gordon's day-7 prediction, Duolingo's "first seven days", Helander's 0–1-photo dropouts, and Laing's steep decline after month 1. STEADY's first-week experience matters more than any long-term feature.
- **What to reward daily:** **"logged today" (ideally multiple meals)**, not "hit the target today". Every large dataset ties success to logging frequency and consistency, and none isolates daily target-hitting as the driver.
- **Speed matters:** "too tedious" (Laing) and entry barriers (Cordeiro) are the named quit reasons, and logging time drops as the habit matures (Harvey). Logging speed is a direct retention lever, which supports one-tap voice and photo logging.

### Gaps
- No public day-by-day retention curve for MyFitnessPal, Lose It, Cal AI or MacroFactor was found.
- No study puts "hit target" and "logged consistently" into the same predictive model, so the inference rests on convergent correlational evidence.
- Jacobs, Radnitz & Hildebrandt (2017), "Adherence as a predictor of weight loss in a commonly used smartphone application" (Obes Res Clin Pract 11(2):206), was found only as a citation. Its findings were not retrieved.

## 2. How do weight GAINERS / bulking lifters differ from weight losers?

### Takeaway
Nearly all tracking-app research studies people *losing* weight. Evidence on gainers is mostly coach and blog opinion.

Recurring gainer pain points:
- **Appetite and fullness** (struggling to eat enough)
- Needing **more frequent meals or liquid calories**
- **Protein adequacy**
- **Slow, noisy progress**

The risk profile also differs. Bulk-and-cut cycles are common among young men and are associated with muscle dysmorphia and ED psychopathology, so "more is always better" framing has its own hazard.

### Cited Findings
- **[OPINION]** Coaching sources put appetite as the main bottleneck. Their advice:
  - eat more often in smaller meals
  - use liquid calories (shakes, smoothies), which suppress the next meal less
  - use calorie-dense foods and cut excess cardio
  - eat roughly 1 g protein per lb of bodyweight
  - [Fitbod](https://fitbod.me/blog/how-to-bulk-if-you-have-a-low-appetite/); [Built With Science](https://builtwithscience.com/fitness-tips/how-to-pack-on-muscle-hardgainer); [Mind Pump](https://mindpumppodcast.com/blog-post/how-to-gain-weight-if-you-are-a-hardgainer/); [Muscle & Strength](https://www.muscleandstrength.com/node/48912)
- **[OPINION]** Typical coach guidance is to gain about 0.5–1 lb/week, or about 1% of bodyweight per month, and to raise intake if the scale trend isn't rising. Coach calorie estimates for "hardgainers" vary widely (about 3,500–4,500 kcal). — [Mind Pump](https://mindpumppodcast.com/blog-post/how-to-gain-weight-if-you-are-a-hardgainer/); [Built With Science](https://builtwithscience.com/fitness-tips/how-to-pack-on-muscle-hardgainer)
- **[PEER] (2022)** "Bulk and cut" study (Eating and Weight Disorders, doi 10.1007/s40519-022-01470-y). Canadian survey of **2,762 people aged 16–30**.
  - **Prevalence:** **48.9% of men**, 21.2% of women and 21.9% of TGNC participants reported bulk-and-cut cycles in the past 12 months.
  - **Associations:** cycling was linked to stronger drive for muscularity, and to more severe ED and muscle-dysmorphia psychopathology in men and women.
  - Cross-sectional. The author list was not confirmed in the search (likely Ganson/Nagata et al.; verify). — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40519-022-01470-y); [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/amp/articles/bulk-and-cut-dieting-linked-to-eating-disorders-muscle-dysmorphia-in-youth)
- **[OPINION] (Jan 2026)** NPR: Jason Nagata (UCSF) estimates **about a third of US teenage boys are trying to bulk up**. Muscle dysmorphia carries elevated risk of disordered eating, steroid use and suicidal ideation. — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/01/14/nx-s1-5671789/bigorexia-dysmorphia-eating-disorder-boys)
- **[PEER, WEAK] (2019)** Linardon & Messer. Among 122 men recruited mostly via fitness social media:
  - **56%** had used MyFitnessPal.
  - Users scored higher on ED attitudes and behaviours, including **binge eating** and **dichotomous thinking**, with large effect sizes.
  - **About 40%** of users felt MFP contributed to disordered eating.
  - This is the sample closest to STEADY's persona. Cross-sectional. — [PubMed 30772765](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30772765/)
- **[INDUSTRY]** MacroFactor treats gain and loss symmetrically. It turns the desired rate of gain into a surplus on top of estimated expenditure and recalculates weekly. — [MacroFactor algorithms & philosophy](https://macrofactor.com/macrofactors-algorithms-and-core-philosophy/)
- **[INDUSTRY]** Yazio offers a "build muscle and gain weight" goal. A review says Yazio encourages keeping calorie intake "as exact as possible", neither below nor above the goal. — [Food & Nutrition Magazine](https://foodandnutrition.org/from-the-magazine/yazio-ios-version-4-6-3/)

### Inferences
- **Failure mode:** for a gainer it is **under-eating**, and "over target" is usually success. The most useful nudge is "you're about 700 kcal short; here's an easy 500 kcal option", not a warning.
- **Noise:** a gainer's weekly change is small relative to day-to-day scale noise (MacroFactor calls bodyweight "noisy"), so **trend smoothing matters even more** than for losers (see section 12).
- **What gainers watch:** protein is the second metric after calories, and strength progression is a natural success signal for lifters.
- **Guardrails:** bulk/cut and muscle-dysmorphia data argue for limits even for gainers. Don't reward unlimited surplus, cap the "surplus zone", and avoid physique-shaming comparisons.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of tracking-app engagement or retention specifically among people trying to gain weight or muscle was found.
- No data on the share of calorie-app users with gain goals. (MyFitnessPal and Lose It analyses filter to loss goals.)
- No India-specific data on gainers' eating patterns or app use was found.

## 3. How precise do users want to be? Precision vs adherence

### Takeaway
Small RCTs suggest **simplified tracking can match full calorie tracking** on adherence and weight outcomes. Effort and tedium are the top stated reasons for lapsing. Time spent logging doesn't predict success, but frequency does. Evidence quality: pilot-scale, with larger trials registered but not yet reported.

### Cited Findings
- **[PEER, WEAK: pilot] (2022)** Nezami et al. (Obesity 30(3):628–638), 6-month pilot RCT in parents with overweight or obesity.
  - **Comparison:** simplified tracking of only high-calorie "red" foods vs full calorie tracking.
  - **Result:** both arms lost clinically meaningful weight, with **no difference in tracking adherence or dietary intake**.
  - Details come via the citing paper and search summary; verify against the original. — [cited in PMC9795401](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9795401/)
- **[PEER, WEAK: pilot] (2022)** Patel et al., "Detailed Versus Simplified Dietary Self-monitoring in a Digital Weight Loss Intervention Among Racial and Ethnic Minority Adults" (JMIR Formative Research 2022;6(12):e42191). A feasibility and acceptability pilot. — [PMC9795401](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9795401/)
- **[PEER]** A Delphi study of experts named three promising abbreviated strategies:
  - **self-weighing only**
  - **tracking only calorie-dense foods**
  - **fewer full-tracking days per week**
  - Larger trials are registered without results (Project THRIVE, NCT05241353; a 328-person "red zone foods vs all calories" trial). — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05241353](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05241353); [Veeva listing](https://ctv.veeva.com/study/simplifying-diet-tracking-in-a-digital-weight-loss-intervention)
- **[PEER] (2014)** Laing: patients found full MyFitnessPal logging "too tedious". — [MDedge](https://www.mdedge.com/content/most-weight-loss-apps-are-losers)
- **[PEER] (2019)** Harvey: time spent logging did not separate successful from unsuccessful participants, but frequency did. — [PubMed 30801989](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30801989/)
- **[INDUSTRY]** MacroFactor:
  - asks users to log "as thoroughly and accurately as is reasonable for you"
  - says **under-counting** is the most common error
  - says not logging during illness or vacation is "completely okay"
  - [MacroFactor philosophy](https://macrofactor.com/macrofactors-algorithms-and-core-philosophy/)
- **[INDUSTRY] (2025)** Wearables are moving toward low-precision logging:
  - **Oura Meals** logs by photo, typed or dictated description, or favourites, and rates meals' components **low/moderate/high**, "without calorie counting (or judgment)". — [Oura blog](https://ouraring.com/blog/oura-meals/); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/oura-launches-meals-and-glucose-features-3554677/)
  - **Bevel** (Feb 2025) went the other way. It emphasises a verified 6M+ food database over pure-LLM estimates, citing LLM inconsistency. — [Fitt Insider press release](https://insider.fitt.co/press-release/bevel-launches-ai-powered-nutrition-app/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ml/app/id6456176249)

### Inferences
- "Rough but frequent" beats "precise but abandoned", which supports STEADY's AI-estimate-plus-quick-confirm flow. Offer gram-level precision as optional and never required.
- An adaptive-expenditure model (section 9) absorbs *systematic* estimation error. If a user consistently under-counts, the back-calculated TDEE shifts to match, which lowers the stakes of portion precision.
- Bevel's positioning matches STEADY's existing architecture (cache, then USDA/INDB, then AI estimate; never LLM-invented macros). It is a credible differentiator against pure photo-AI apps.

### Gaps
- No survey data on what share of users weigh food vs estimate was found.
- No head-to-head RCT of photo/AI logging vs manual database logging on adherence was found in this pass.

## 4. Ring psychology: goal-gradient, closure/completion, Zeigarnik, endowed progress

### Takeaway
The ring mechanic rests on well-replicated **goal-gradient** and **endowed-progress** findings. The popular Zeigarnik story ("unfinished tasks nag at you") is weaker: a 2025 meta-analysis found no general memory effect, though people do reliably tend to **resume** interrupted tasks (Ovsiankina effect).

**No peer-reviewed study tests Apple's rings causally.** Apple's own framing is "the least you should do every day", not a maximum.

### Cited Findings
- **[PEER] (2006)** Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng, "The Goal-Gradient Hypothesis Resurrected" (J. Marketing Research 43:39–58).
  - **Effort accelerates as people near a goal**, as a function of the *proportion* of distance remaining.
  - "Illusionary goal progress" sped up purchases and increased retention in a rewards program.
  - The effect wasn't explained by habituation or time trends.
  - [Columbia Business School](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/goal-gradient-hypothesis-resurrected-purchase-acceleration-illusionary-goal)
- **[PEER] (2006)** Nunes & Drèze, "The Endowed Progress Effect".
  - Artificial head-start progress (a 10-stamp card with 2 pre-stamped vs an 8-stamp card) **raised completion and sped it up**.
  - The effect depends on perceived completion, not on avoiding waste.
  - Car-wash redemption is often reported as **34% vs 19%**, via secondary summary; verify. — [Paper PDF (USC)](https://msbfile03.usc.edu/digitalmeasures/jnunes/intellcont/Endowed%20Progress%20Effect-1.pdf)
- **[PEER] (2025)** Ghibellini & Meier meta-analysis of the Zeigarnik and Ovsiankina effects (Humanities & Social Sciences Communications, vol. 12; about 59 publications).
  - **No general memory advantage for unfinished tasks:** Zeigarnik "lacks universal validity".
  - **A general tendency to resume interrupted tasks:** Ovsiankina holds.
  - [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html); [Univ. Bern PDF](https://www.kog.psy.unibe.ch/e48276/e1785865/s41599-025-05000-w1_ger.pdf)
- **[PEER] (2016)** Etkin, "The Hidden Cost of Personal Quantification" (JCR 42(6):967–984), 6 experiments.
  - Measuring **increases how much people do but reduces their enjoyment**.
  - It can lower continued engagement and wellbeing once tracking stops, even for people who chose to be measured.
  - [Wharton PDF](https://marketing.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Etkin-Jordan-11-12-15-Hidden-Cost.pdf); [Duke Fuqua](https://www.fuqua.duke.edu/duke-fuqua-insights/etkin-counting-steps)
- **[INDUSTRY] (2015)** Apple's Jay Blahnik on the rings:
  - The guiding principle was "Sit less, move more, and get some exercise". Apple rejected fitness "highjacked to be about one number", and three rings show "the whole picture at a glance". — [9to5Mac memo](https://9to5mac.com/2015/03/09/jay-blahnik-memo/)
  - The team tried hundreds of interface designs before the rings. — [Engadget 2015](https://www.engadget.com/2015-03-10-apple-watch-activity-design.html)
  - The rings represent **"the least you should do every day" rather than the most**. A goal that feels like a daily challenge leads to burnout, while simple routine activity becomes a ritual. — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/jay-blahnik-what-separates-apple-watch-other-fitness-trackers)
  - Later: "Measurement is motivation". — [T3](https://t3.com/active/a-decade-of-apple-fitness-with-jay-blahnik-were-excited-about-ais-role-in-health-and-fitness)
- **[INDUSTRY, WEAK]** Apple Heart and Movement Study (>140,000 participants): people who closed rings "most of the time" were **48% less likely to report poor sleep quality** than infrequent closers. Correlational, since active people both close rings and sleep better. — [Galaxus/Digitec](https://www.galaxus.nl/en/page/why-am-i-so-obsessed-with-closing-my-rings-37588)
- **[OPINION]** Prof. Yang Wei (Nottingham Trent): rings work "because it turns behavior change into a simple daily goal system and gives frequent feedback about progress", but "those already active may not need it". — [Galaxus/Digitec](https://www.galaxus.nl/en/page/why-am-i-so-obsessed-with-closing-my-rings-37588)
- **[PEER, WEAK] (2024)** Arnold, Germelmann, Kuhn & Henze ran a field study on whether closing the activity ring causes *adverse* effects in smartwatch use (AMS World Marketing Congress). Results weren't accessible. — [Univ. Bayreuth eRef](https://eref.uni-bayreuth.de/90156)

### Inferences
- **Goal-gradient:** rings make the last stretch feel urgent. Make that last stretch achievable, e.g., "one more meal logged closes it".
- **Endowed progress:** start the consistency ring with partial credit, e.g., logging breakfast fills a third, rather than showing an empty ring every morning.
- **Ovsiankina, not Zeigarnik:** an open ring works as a *resumption prompt* at a natural re-entry point (an evening "1 meal left to close" nudge), not as background guilt.
- **Minimum, not maximum:** follow Blahnik's framing. Rings should mark a sensible daily floor (log meals, reach the protein floor, get into the calorie zone), not a stretch goal.
- **Etkin's warning:** heavy quantification can erode enjoyment of food. Frame rings as fuel for the user's goal, not as a test score.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed causal study of Activity rings (or any ring UI) on behaviour or retention was found.
- No published Apple data on ring-closure rates or on how pause/rest-day features changed engagement.

## 5. Which 3 ring metrics, and how do other apps show over/under?

### Takeaway
Calorie and macro trackers converge on **calorie and macro rings or bars**, and many treat over-target as red or bad, which is wrong for a gainer. **MacroFactor is the clearest "adherence-neutral" exception: it never turns numbers red.** Oura avoids calories entirely (low/moderate/high labels). No mainstream app was found that runs Apple-style "closeable" nutrition rings with goal-direction-aware colouring, so STEADY's concept looks open.

### Cited Findings
- **[INDUSTRY]** MacroFactor: "MacroFactor doesn't turn any numbers red… The numbers on the screen change, but that's it: just neutral information with no implied judgments attached." It does not force users to "make up for" deviations. — [MacroFactor philosophy](https://macrofactor.com/macrofactors-algorithms-and-core-philosophy/)
- **[INDUSTRY]** Oura Meals labels meal components **low/moderate/high** and aims to help "without calorie counting (or judgment)". It is now a permanent feature (previously Oura Labs). It isn't available on Gen2 rings and is English-only. — [Oura blog](https://ouraring.com/blog/oura-meals/); [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/wearables/articles/oura-adds-two-ai-features-202900855.html)
- **[INDUSTRY]** Bevel launched AI nutrition tracking in Feb 2025: barcode, image, recipe, "describe meal", a 6M+ food database, and Dexcom/Libre CGM integration. No ring-style nutrition display was found. — [Fitt Insider](https://insider.fitt.co/press-release/bevel-launches-ai-powered-nutrition-app/)
- **[INDUSTRY, WEAK]** WHOOP: no native nutrition logging was found as of the search. Users request AI photo logging and protein tracking, and nutrition comes in via MyFitnessPal/Apple Health or third parties (Plait, Pivot). — [WHOOP community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/product-suggestion-native-ai-nutrition-tracking-inside-whoop/14901)
- **[INDUSTRY, WEAK]** Smaller apps:
  - CalorieBro shows protein, carb and fat progress as rings. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/caloriebro-calorie-tracker/id6754843196)
  - "Protein Ring" makes protein the hero ring, with calories/carbs/fat/fiber secondary. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6785971667)
  - One app's calorie ring "turns red once you pass your target". — [DailySehat](https://dailysehat-web.onrender.com/features)
- **[INDUSTRY]** Cal AI estimates calories and macros from photo or barcode, sets a calorie goal from weight/height/activity, and sends logging reminders. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/py/app/cal-ai-my-calorie-counter-app/id6737433504)
- **[INDUSTRY] (2015)** Apple deliberately combined an *outcome* ring (Move), an *intensity* ring (Exercise) and a *frequency/behaviour* ring (Stand). — [9to5Mac memo](https://9to5mac.com/2015/03/09/jay-blahnik-memo/)

### Inferences
- **Candidate trio for STEADY:**
  1. **Calories, as a goal-aware zone.** For a gainer, the ring closes on entering the zone, and above-zone shows as "surplus", not red.
  2. **Protein, as a floor goal** ("at least X g").
  3. **Consistency/meals logged, as a behaviour ring.**
- **Why that trio:** it mirrors Apple's outcome + quality + behaviour structure. Section 1 says the behaviour ring is the best predictor of success, and it is the one ring a user can always close, even on an off-plan day.
- **Why not water:** it is low-evidence and adds logging burden.
- **Why not fat/carbs as rings:** they suit a detail view better than a ring (Blahnik's "whole picture at a glance").

### Gaps
- Lifesum and Yazio over-goal display behaviour couldn't be confirmed from public sources. Their help centres or a hands-on teardown would settle it.
- No retention or outcome data comparing ring vs bar vs number displays for nutrition was found.

## 6. Fixed target vs range/zone goals

### Takeaway
**Range goals ("lose 2–4 lb") produce higher re-engagement than single-number goals ("lose 3 lb") with equal outcomes**, via a feeling of accomplishment. Goals with built-in slack ("emergency reserves") are *preferred* and produce *more persistence after failure*. Rigid one-size targets show an effort-adherence trade-off. Together this supports a calorie **zone** over a single number.

### Cited Findings
- **[PEER] (2013)** Scott & Nowlis, "The Effect of Goal Specificity on Consumer Goal Reengagement" (JCR 40(3):444–459).
  - People with **high-low range goals were more likely to re-engage**, e.g., re-enrol in a weight-loss program, **even though average weight loss didn't differ** from single-number goals.
  - Mechanism: feelings of accomplishment, because the low end feels attainable and the high end feels challenging.
  - It replicated for resisting tempting foods, puzzles and a grocery game.
  - [WashU press release](https://source.washu.edu/2013/06/why-is-it-easier-to-lose-24-pounds-rather-than-3-pounds/); [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/doi10.1086-670766.html)
- **[PEER] (2017/2019)** Sharif & Shu, emergency reserves (J. Marketing Research 54(3), 2017; Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes, 2019).
  - **Diet-points choice (n=100):** a "30 points + 2 emergency points" plan was chosen over Easy (32) by **46% vs 29%**, and over Hard (30) by **46% vs 25%**.
  - **Word-search choice:** a range-hard goal (**66%**) beat the reserve goal when those two went head to head.
  - **Month-long pedometer study (n>270):** reserve groups hit goals **up to 40% more often** than Easy or Hard and walked more steps.
  - Summarised by UCLA Anderson Review. — [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/emergency-reserves/); [OBHDP paper PDF](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Sharif-Shu_EmergencyReserveFailure_OBHDP2019.pdf)
- **[PEER, WEAK] (2003)** 92 sedentary women: a fixed 10,000-step goal vs a personalised goal produced similar step counts, but **lower adherence among low-activity women given the fixed target** (an "effort-adherence trade-off"). Conference abstract. — [AAHPERD 2003](https://aahperd.confex.com/aahperd/2003/finalprogram/paper_3444.htm)
- **[INDUSTRY] (2015)** Blahnik rejected fitness "highjacked to be about one number". — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2015/03/09/jay-blahnik-memo/)

### Inferences
- Show calories as a **target zone**, e.g., a gainer at 2,800–3,200 kcal: entering the zone closes the ring, and ≥3,200 shows as "surplus achieved", capped at a sensible ceiling.
- A protein floor is already a range with an open top.
- Sharif & Shu's 66% result suggests a **range with a challenging top end** is the most attractive frame.

### Gaps
- No study of range vs point goals specifically inside a daily calorie-tracking UI was found.

## 7. Rewards: streaks, badges, share cards, celebrations

### Takeaway
Streaks are the best-evidenced engagement mechanic, but they work best when the bar is **minimal** (Duolingo's "one lesson" change) and when broken streaks are **not** dwelt on. Gamification has real effects that **shrink** after interventions end. Tangible, contingent rewards can undermine intrinsic motivation, while informational positive feedback enhances it.

### Cited Findings
- **[INDUSTRY]** Duolingo "Improving the streak". The streak used to require hitting the daily XP goal; it was changed so **one lesson extends the streak**.
  - **Retention:** **+3.3% relative D14 retention** and **+1% DAU**.
  - **Streak uptake:** **+10.5%** more daily learners on a streak within 20 days (+19% among new learners), and **over 40% more learners on 7+ day streaks** a year later.
  - **Cost:** fewer learners reached their daily goals.
  - **Rationale:** learners with "intense" goals were *less* likely to keep streaks. Almost 40% of learners who were active 2 days in a row without a streak had the intense goal.
  - [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)
- **[INDUSTRY]** Duolingo "How the streak builds habit":
  - 7-day-streak learners are **3.6× more likely to complete their course** (correlational).
  - A milestone animation raised new-learner D7 retention by **+1.7%**.
  - Allowing **2 equipped Streak Freezes instead of 1** raised DAU by **+0.38%**.
  - Early streak thrill fades and motivation shifts to **loss aversion** as streaks lengthen.
  - The freeze concept rests on UPenn/UCLA research that **slack** motivates more than rigid rules (Sharif & Shu, section 6).
  - [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)
- **[PEER] (2023)** Silverman & Barasch, "On or Off Track: How (Broken) Streaks Affect Consumer Decisions" (JCR 49(6):1095–1117), 7 studies.
  - **Highlighting intact streaks increases subsequent engagement more than highlighting broken ones**, and keeping a logged streak becomes a goal in itself.
  - The break hurts more when people **blame themselves**, and less when they can **repair** it.
  - Barasch (interview): highlighting a recent failure with a nudge to get back on track "typically doesn't work".
  - [UD Space](https://udspace.udel.edu/items/42ce576b-8e1f-429a-8541-e29e48dbcfbb); [CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/business/news/2023/04/20/research-streaks-marketing-tech-barasch)
- **[PEER] (1999)** Deci, Koestner & Ryan meta-analysis (Psychological Bulletin, 128 studies). Effects on free-choice intrinsic motivation:
  - **Undermined by** engagement-contingent rewards (**d = −0.40**), completion-contingent (**−0.36**) and performance-contingent (**−0.28**).
  - **Positive (informational) feedback enhanced it** (**d = +0.33** for free-choice behaviour, **+0.31** for interest).
  - The debate continues (Cameron et al. 2001). — [Meta-analysis PDF (UW)](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf)
- **[PEER] (2022)** Mazeas et al., meta-analysis of gamification RCTs for physical activity (JMIR). Significant short-term effect. At about 14 weeks after the intervention ended the effect was **weaker, "very small to small"**, but persisted, which the authors argue rules out a pure novelty effect. — [JMIR 2022 / DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/9754f1fd9dd84266b5e5a30336858ad8)
- **[PEER] (2025)** Gamification for cardiovascular patients: maintenance effect **g = 0.20 (95% CI 0.12–0.29; prediction interval −0.01 to 0.41)** from 3 studies with about 2.5-month follow-up. Uncertain. — [JMIR Serious Games 2025](https://games.jmir.org/article/export/end/games_v13i1e64410)
- **[PEER] (2016)** Etkin: measurement raises activity but lowers enjoyment, and continued engagement can drop once measurement stops. — [Wharton PDF](https://marketing.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Etkin-Jordan-11-12-15-Hidden-Cost.pdf)

### Inferences
- Tie the streak to **"logged at least one meal"** (Duolingo lesson), never to "hit calorie target".
- **Celebrate intact streaks and milestones** (day 3, day 7). Avoid "you broke your streak" screens, and offer repair or freeze.
- Prefer **informational praise** ("Protein floor hit 5 of 7 days, your best week") over tangible or point rewards (Deci et al.).
- Expect any badge or celebration effect to decay, so the core logging loop must stand on its own speed and usefulness.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or first-party data on **share cards** or social sharing for nutrition apps was found.
- Duolingo's streak-wager, earn-back and "perfect streak" results are not public (only chapter titles in the Lenny's podcast).

## 8. Streak resets vs freezes/rest days; the what-the-hell effect

### Takeaway
All-or-nothing framing invites the **"what-the-hell" effect**: the *belief* of having failed, not actual intake, triggers disinhibited eating, usually for the rest of the day. Highlighting broken streaks lowers re-engagement. **Slack/reserve days raise next-day rebound after a miss** (55% vs 37% under rigid goals). Apple itself added ring pausing and per-day goals in 2024.

### Cited Findings
- **[PEER] (2010)** Polivy, Herman & Deo. Everyone ate the same pizza, but when it was *presented* as a large slice, **dieters then ate almost twice as many cookies** as non-dieters. There was no effect in the small or regular conditions, so the *perception* of having blown the diet triggers counter-regulatory eating. Polivy: it "usually lasts for the day". — [Wikipedia: Counterregulatory eating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterregulatory_eating); [WTOP](https://wtop.com/news/2017/11/avoiding-the-what-the-hell-health-effect). [Secondary summaries; verify.]
- **[PEER, WEAK]** Higher self-compassion after a dietary lapse is associated with better control of weight-management behaviour in the following hours. — [WTOP](https://wtop.com/news/2017/11/avoiding-the-what-the-hell-health-effect). [Secondary; original not retrieved.]
- **[PEER] (2019)** Sharif & Shu month-long pedometer study (n>270). Probability of hitting the goal **the day after a miss**:

  | Condition | Rebound rate |
  |---|---|
  | Reserve-weekly (2 skips/week) | **55%** |
  | Reserve-monthly (8 skips/month) | **47%** |
  | Easy | **44%** |
  | Hard (7/7 days) | **37%** |

  In another study (n=1,200), persistence after a reserve-goal failure was "nearly identical" to after success. — [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/emergency-reserves/)
- **[PEER] (2023)** Being able to "repair" a broken streak softens the demotivation, and self-attributed breaks hurt most. — [Silverman & Barasch](https://udspace.udel.edu/items/42ce576b-8e1f-429a-8541-e29e48dbcfbb)
- **[INDUSTRY] (2024)** Apple watchOS 11 added:
  - **Pause Rings** (today, a week, a month, up to 90 days) **without losing award streaks**, with coaching notifications muted
  - **per-day-of-week goals** for each ring
  - Press framed this as Apple acknowledging that rigid daily rings caused guilt. — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2024/07/16/close-your-ringsbut-in-watchos-11-its-okay-if-you-dont/); [BGR](https://bgr.com/tech/with-watchos-11-pausing-my-activity-rings-wont-make-me-feel-bad-about-myself-anymore/); [iPhoneLife](https://iphonelife.com/content/customize-activity-ring-goals-day)
- **[INDUSTRY]** Duolingo: allowing 2 freezes instead of 1 gave **+0.38% DAU**. — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)
- **[PEER] (2017)** Simpson & Mazzeo speculated that activity tracking without rest days may encourage excessive exercise. — [Psychology in Action](https://www.psychologyinaction.org/2018-4-29-calorie-counting-and-fitness-tracking-technology-associations-with-eating-disorder-symptomology/)

### Inferences
- **Streak logic:** only "did you log" counts. An off-target day never "breaks" anything.
- **Slack:** build about **2 grace days per week** (Sharif & Shu's best-rebounding condition) plus an explicit pause for travel or illness, modelled on watchOS 11.
- **After a big off-plan day:** respond with neutral, forward-looking copy ("Logged. Tomorrow's a fresh ring."), never failure language. This avoids the what-the-hell spiral and the self-attribution amplifier.

### Gaps
- The classic abstinence violation effect (Marlatt & Gordon, 1985) was not retrieved from a primary or authoritative source this pass.
- No public data on retention impact of Apple's Pause Rings.

## 9. Adaptive targets (MacroFactor, Apple Move goal)

### Takeaway
MacroFactor back-calculates expenditure from logged intake and smoothed trend weight and adjusts targets weekly. It is explicitly "adherence-neutral" and settles in about 14–30 days. Users complain about Apple's ever-rising Move-goal suggestions, but the evidence is anecdotal. Apple's own philosophy ("least you should do", "daily challenge leads to burnout") argues against aggressive auto-increases.

### Cited Findings
- **[INDUSTRY]** MacroFactor's algorithm:
  - Solves energy balance for "calories out" from intake and weight change.
  - Uses a recency-weighted, long-window trend weight, so it largely ignores 1–4-day blips.
  - Converts the desired gain or loss rate into a surplus or deficit.
  - The estimate settles after **about 14–30 days**, and each weekly check-in is "its own self-contained unit".
  - After logging gaps it carries forward the last "high-confidence" expenditure. Users can skip check-ins after unusual weeks.
  - Known failure modes: creatine, carb or fiber shifts, and big temporary activity spikes (1–2 weeks of bad adjustments).
  - [MacroFactor philosophy](https://macrofactor.com/macrofactors-algorithms-and-core-philosophy/); [MacroFactor expenditure page](https://macrofactorapp.com/?p=4961)
- **[INDUSTRY]** A MacroFactor-cited case: actual expenditure was **700 kcal/day above** the formula estimate, and the algorithm caught it in **about 20 days**. — [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=53698)
- **[OPINION, WEAK]** A reviewer says MacroFactor needs about 2 weeks of consistent data to stabilise. — [calorie-trackers.com](https://calorie-trackers.com/reviews/macrofactor/)
- **[OPINION]** MacroFactor reports users saying they log more consistently since switching, and one user called it "depressingly revolutionary" (anecdotal). — [MacroFactor philosophy](https://macrofactor.com/macrofactors-algorithms-and-core-philosophy/)
- **[OPINION, WEAK]** Apple Watch users report the weekly Move-goal suggestion rising whenever they did well, with "no upper limit", and call it unhealthy. — [iMore forum](https://forums.imore.com/apple-watch/339518-new-daily-move-suggestion-impossible-reach.html)
- **[INDUSTRY]** Blahnik: a goal that feels like a daily challenge leads to burnout. — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/jay-blahnik-what-separates-apple-watch-other-fitness-trackers)
- **[PEER] (2025)** UCL/Loughborough: "disappointment at slow progress toward algorithm-generated targets" was a major negative theme (section 11). — [UCL news](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2025/oct/emotional-strain-fitness-and-calorie-counting-apps-revealed)

### Inferences
- Adaptive TDEE suits gainers, whose formula TDEE is often underestimated (anecdotal 700 kcal case).
- Design rules:
  - Adjust **weekly, not daily**.
  - Explain every change in plain words ("Your weight trend rose 0.1 kg/wk vs the 0.25 target, so we're adding 150 kcal").
  - Cap the step size, and require user confirmation.
  - Never ratchet up merely because the user succeeded.
- Expect about 2–4 weeks before targets settle, so the first month shouldn't promise precision.

### Gaps
- No independent peer-reviewed validation of MacroFactor's algorithm, and no retention data, was found.
- Apple's Move-goal suggestion logic isn't publicly documented.

## 10. Daily vs weekly framing (weekly budgets, calorie cycling)

### Takeaway
There is **no strong evidence that weekly budgets or planned flexibility reduce dropout** compared with continuous daily targets in weight-loss trials. What does help is **slack within a daily structure** (emergency reserves, best with weekly skips). Daily goals appear to be preferred for motivation (weak evidence). Best fit: daily rings for the habit, plus a weekly lens for judging calorie outcomes.

### Cited Findings
- **[PEER]** A planned-pause systematic review (Wren et al., per search summary) covered **9 arms, 796 participants** with planned diet pauses of at least 1 week vs continuous restriction. **No evidence that pauses reduced attrition**, and weight loss was no better or worse. — [PMC10255792](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10255792/)
- **[PEER]** Earlier reviews of intermittent vs continuous energy restriction reported **comparable weight loss and similar attrition**, despite expectations that flexibility would help. — [Oxford ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:2be439e4-82f1-4ae8-a8b8-5269f75635eb/files/s3n204143b)
- **[PEER]** BMJ network meta-analysis of 99 trials: alternate-day fasting gave only **−1.29 kg** extra loss vs continuous restriction (below a 2 kg clinical threshold). — [BMJ](https://www.bmj.com/node/1111119)
- **[PEER] (2017/2019)** Sharif & Shu: the **reserve-weekly** frame (daily goal with 2 skips per week, no rollover) produced the best rebound after a miss (**55%** vs 37% for a rigid daily goal). — [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/emergency-reserves/)
- **[PEER, WEAK] (2024)** University of Twente single-case thesis (Fitbit, 6 weeks): daily adaptive step goals produced a significant upward trend (p=0.037) where weekly goals did not, and **80% preferred daily goals**. — [Univ. Twente](https://essay.utwente.nl/essays/101038)
- **[INDUSTRY]** MacroFactor treats each week as a self-contained unit and never forces "make-up" deficits after over-eating. — [MacroFactor philosophy](https://macrofactor.com/macrofactors-algorithms-and-core-philosophy/)
- **[INDUSTRY] (2024)** watchOS 11 allows **different ring goals per day of week**, effectively built-in calorie or activity cycling. — [iPhoneLife](https://iphonelife.com/content/customize-activity-ring-goals-day)
- **[OPINION, WEAK]** A BMJ commentary citing a 5:2 trial reports that adherence to continuous restriction fell from about 30% of the target deficit at 3 months to about 9.5% at 12 months. Indicative only. — [BMJ](https://www.bmj.com/node/1111119)

### Inferences
- **Daily rings, weekly verdict:** keep daily rings (habit, Duolingo-style), but judge calorie *outcome* over the week ("5 of 7 days in your surplus zone"). That keeps one off day from becoming a failure.
- **Weekend flex:** optional per-day targets (higher on training days or weekends) match watchOS 11 and Indian social-eating patterns. Don't make debt or "make-up" mechanics mandatory, since MacroFactor deliberately avoids them.

### Gaps
- No RCT directly comparing a *weekly calorie budget UI* vs a *daily target UI* on app adherence was found.
- The Wren review's attribution and exact attrition numbers came from a search summary; verify against the full text.

## 11. Negative/shame feedback, red warnings, and eating-disorder risk

### Takeaway
Users' most-voiced complaints about fitness and calorie apps are **shame, notification irritation, and demotivation from slow progress against algorithmic targets**. Cross-sectional studies link calorie-app use to ED symptoms, especially in clinical and fitness-oriented samples. The one RCT (n=200, 1 month) found **no harm** in non-clinical women, and daily-weighing RCTs found no rise in anorectic cognitions or bingeing. Red's "avoidance" effect on performance is contested. The industry trend is neutral or no-judgment displays.

### Cited Findings
- **[PEER] (Oct 2025)** UCL/Loughborough, *British Journal of Health Psychology*. **58,881 tweets** about 5 popular fitness apps; AI identified **13,799 negative posts**.
  - **Themes:** **shame about logging unhealthy food**, irritation at notifications, and disappointment at slow progress toward algorithm-generated targets.
  - **Outcome:** users reported **"demotivation"** and giving up. One exercised out of guilt about a missed calorie goal.
  - **Recommendation:** move from rigid calorie counting to holistic, wellbeing- and enjoyment-focused design.
  - **Limits:** only negative posts were analysed, so there's no net-effect estimate. Co-author Dr Lucy Porter notes the apps likely benefit many.
  - [UCL news, 22 Oct 2025](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2025/oct/emotional-strain-fitness-and-calorie-counting-apps-revealed); [Scimex](https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/are-fitness-apps-doing-more-harm-than-good)
- **[PEER, WEAK] (2017)** Levinson, Fewell & Brosof (Eating Behaviors 27:14–16), 105 people with diagnosed EDs.
  - **About 75% used MyFitnessPal, and 73% of users felt it contributed to their ED.** Those perceptions correlated with symptom severity.
  - Cross-sectional, clinical sample. — [PubMed 28843591](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28843591/)
- **[PEER, WEAK] (2017)** Simpson & Mazzeo (Eating Behaviors 26:89–92), 493 undergraduates.
  - Calorie-tracking use was linked to **anxious food attitudes and restrictive dieting**.
  - **Fitness tracking, not calorie tracking**, uniquely indicated overall ED symptoms.
  - Cross-sectional. — [Psychology in Action](https://www.psychologyinaction.org/2018-4-29-calorie-counting-and-fitness-tracking-technology-associations-with-eating-disorder-symptomology/); [PMC5700836](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5700836/)
- **[PEER, WEAK] (2019)** Linardon & Messer: in men from fitness communities, MyFitnessPal users had higher ED attitudes and behaviours (large effects), and about 40% perceived a contribution. — [PubMed 30772765](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30772765/)
- **[PEER] (2021)** Hahn et al. RCT (J Acad Nutr Diet 121(12):2377–2388; NCT04052529). **200 female undergraduates** not already monitoring their diet were randomised to 1 month of MyFitnessPal tracking or control. **No significant negative effects** on:
  - ED risk, anxiety or depression
  - body satisfaction or quality of life
  - eating behaviours or other self-monitoring
  - [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04052529); [Stronger By Science review](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=55346)
- **[PEER] (2013)** Steinberg et al. daily-weighing RCT (n=91): **no significant differences** in depressive symptoms, anorectic cognitions, disinhibition, hunger susceptibility or binge eating vs control. — [UNC Gillings](https://sph.unc.edu/?p=62844); [Duke Global Health](https://globalhealth.duke.edu/news/daily-self-weighing-helps-study-participants-lose-weight)
- **[PEER, contested] (2007)** Elliot et al.: seeing red before or during achievement tasks impaired performance and triggered avoidance motivation. However, **independent direct replications failed**, and Elliot's group later acknowledged the evidence is "not as unequivocal" as first suggested. — [APA Monitor](https://www.apa.org/monitor/may07/seeingred); [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2007/02/070228170240.htm); [Steele replication poster 2015](https://www.appstate.edu/~steelekm/documents/Psychonomics2015Poster_Steele.pdf); [Steele 2017](https://www.appstate.edu/~steelekm/documents/Psychonomic2017Poster_Steele.pdf)
- **[INDUSTRY]** Two design responses to judgment:
  - MacroFactor never turns numbers red ("no implied judgments"). — [MacroFactor](https://macrofactor.com/macrofactors-algorithms-and-core-philosophy/)
  - Oura Meals avoids calories and "judgment" altogether. — [Oura blog](https://ouraring.com/blog/oura-meals/)
- **[PEER] (2023)** Self-attributed failure amplifies the demotivating effect of broken streaks. — [Silverman & Barasch](https://udspace.udel.edu/items/42ce576b-8e1f-429a-8541-e29e48dbcfbb)
- **[OPINION]** Commentary across sources suggests the *motive* for tracking (appearance-driven vs health-driven), more than tracking itself, links tracking to ED pathology. — [Examine.com](https://examine.com/conditions/disordered-eating/faq/does-macronutrient-tracking-cause-disordered-eating)

### Inferences
- Risk concentrates in already-vulnerable users and appearance- or muscularity-driven motives. Short-term tracking in general populations showed no harm in the one RCT.
- Mitigations for STEADY:
  - **No red "over budget" states.** Use neutral or goal-aware colours, because a gainer's surplus is success.
  - **No failure language.**
  - **Calm, sparse notifications**, given the irritation theme.
  - **An option to hide numbers or pause**.
  - **Cap rewards for extreme surplus or deficit**.
  - A gentle off-ramp or resource link if logging patterns look extreme. (Gap: no validated in-app screening approach was found.)
- On colour itself, the evidence is shaky either way. The stronger argument against red is semantic: red means "bad", and for a gainer, being over is good.

### Gaps
- No validated, published in-app ED-risk screening or "safe mode" design for calorie apps was found.
- No RCT longer than 1 month on calorie-app use and ED outcomes, and none in men or gainers.
- The UCL/Loughborough paper's full text and the 5 named apps were not retrieved (press release only).

## 12. How to show 30-day success

### Takeaway
Use a **smoothed weight trend** rather than raw daily weights. Pair it with **consistency stats** (days logged, protein-floor days) and, for lifters, **strength progress**. Celebrate early wins in the first week, since early progress predicts long-term success. Avoid framing that invites "disappointment at slow progress". Progress photos have no evidence base in the sources found.

### Cited Findings
- **[PEER] (2013)** Steinberg et al. daily-weighing RCT (n=91, 6 months). The package was a smart scale, a web weight graph, and weekly tailored feedback emails.
  - **Weight:** **6.55% vs 0.35%** loss.
  - **Clinically meaningful loss:** **42.6% vs 6.8%** reached 5% loss, and **27.7% vs 0%** reached 10%.
  - **Adherence:** participants weighed on **6.1 vs 1.1 days per week**.
  - **Harm:** none in psychological measures.
  - **Limit:** the trial can't isolate the graph's effect from the emails. — [UNC Gillings](https://sph.unc.edu/?p=62844); [Duke Global Health](https://globalhealth.duke.edu/news/daily-self-weighing-helps-study-participants-lose-weight)
- **[INDUSTRY]** MacroFactor uses a recency-weighted smoothed trend weight to separate real tissue change from water and gut-content noise, and short deviations are mostly ignored. — [MacroFactor philosophy](https://macrofactor.com/macrofactors-algorithms-and-core-philosophy/)
- **[PEER] (2019)** Gordon et al.: early (first-week) weight change and behaviour predict long-term goal achievement. — [arXiv 1904.02813](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1904.02813)
- **[PEER] (2025)** UCL/Loughborough: "disappointment at slow progress toward algorithm-generated targets" demotivates. — [UCL news](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2025/oct/emotional-strain-fitness-and-calorie-counting-apps-revealed)
- **[PEER] (2013)** Scott & Nowlis: *feelings of accomplishment* drive re-engagement, and range goals generate more of them at equal outcomes. — [WashU](https://source.washu.edu/2013/06/why-is-it-easier-to-lose-24-pounds-rather-than-3-pounds/)
- **[INDUSTRY]** Duolingo: the 7-day streak milestone is the key early threshold (2.4× next-day return; 3.6× course completion), and milestone animations add +1.7% D7 retention. — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit); [Improving the streak](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)
- **[PEER] (1999)** Informational positive feedback raises intrinsic motivation (d ≈ +0.3). — [Deci et al.](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf)
- **[OPINION]** For gainers, coaches use the scale trend (aiming for about 0.5–1 lb/week or about 1% of bodyweight per month) as the main adjustment signal. — [Mind Pump](https://mindpumppodcast.com/blog-post/how-to-gain-weight-if-you-are-a-hardgainer/)

### Inferences
- A 30-day recap for STEADY could lead with:
  1. **Trend weight vs goal rate**, as a smoothed line (e.g., "+0.9 kg trend, on pace for your +0.25 kg/wk goal")
  2. **Consistency**: days logged and rings closed
  3. **Protein-floor days**
  4. **Optional strength PRs** for lifters
- Show raw daily weights only on demand.
- The day-7 milestone matters more than day 30 for retention, so put a "first week" recap at day 7.
- Use range language for outcomes ("within your target range") to boost accomplishment.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed evidence was found on **progress photos**, or on strength metrics in nutrition apps, as motivators or retention drivers.
- No study isolates the effect of a *smoothed trend graph* vs raw weights on motivation. Steinberg bundles the graph with emails.
