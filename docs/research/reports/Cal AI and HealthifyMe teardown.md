# Win the Trust Cal AI and Healthify Squandered

Cal AI and Healthify are strong in opposite places and fail in the same one: users stop trusting their numbers, their prices and their motives. **Cal AI** wins on ease. One camera has photo, barcode and label modes, a log takes two or three taps, and even one-star reviewers praise the ring-based home screen. It has **4.8 stars from about 375,000 US ratings** and reportedly earned **$30M+ a year** before MyFitnessPal bought it. But it gives the same meal different numbers on different scans, and its "Fix Issue" box can change fields the user never touched. In an NIH weighed-meal test it **under-counted by 345 kcal per meal, the worst of four apps**. Apple pulled it from the App Store for about four days in April 2026 over its billing design. **Healthify** (formerly HealthifyMe) is India's incumbent. It has a food database it started building with India's National Institute of Nutrition in 2012, katori and bowl units, Hinglish voice, and an AI-plus-human coaching model. A 65,000-user Stanford study links the human coaching to more logging. But its free tracker now works mainly as a sales funnel for coaching. Users report **up to 15–20 sales calls a day**, Hindi and English duplicates of the same food with different calories, photo results they cannot edit, slow loading, and Apple Health write-back switched off. **58% of its 150 newest Indian App Store reviews are one or two stars.** Journable, the chat-first tracker studied earlier, fits the same pattern: easy to use, numbers written by a language model, and a paywall users didn't see coming. None of the three gives the same number for the same meal, lets a correction teach the app, writes nutrition data back to the phone's health store, or charges honestly enough to keep billing out of its one-star reviews. Those four gaps are STEADY's opening, and most of the founder's decisions already aim at them. The hard part is matching Healthify's Indian dish coverage, because coverage is what Indian reviewers say stops them from leaving. One finding changes a priority. Every competitor under-counts, but STEADY's numbers run high, and for someone trying to gain weight over-counting is the harmful direction: the ring says they banked a surplus when they actually ate at maintenance. The evidence also argues for a second look at six smaller decisions, covered in the decisions section.

## Store stars measure prompting; written reviews measure pain

Both apps show a large gap between their headline rating and what recent reviewers write, and that gap shapes how to read everything below. In a sample of **1,007 recent written Cal AI reviews** from five App Store countries, the average was **2.2 to 2.9 stars in the US, Canada, UK and Australia, and 3.4 in India**. The overall rating is 4.8 ([US feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json); [iTunes Lookup US](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6480417616&country=us)). Of Healthify's 150 most recent Indian App Store reviews, **87 are one or two stars**, against an overall 4.55. Its Trustpilot score is 2.4 ([App Store IN feed](https://itunes.apple.com/in/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=943712366/sortBy=mostRecent/json); [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.healthifyme.com)). Part of the gap is normal, because angry users write and happy users tap a star. Part is engineered. Cal AI asks for an App Store rating on **screen 23 of its onboarding, before the user has logged a single meal** ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/apps/cal-ai-calorie-tracker/?id=1739&ts=0&vt=1)). More than 35 five-star Healthify reviews naming specific coaches arrived in under a week in July 2026. That looks like solicited reviews, though no evidence of incentives was found. So the counts of complaint themes below show what people complain about, not what share of users are unhappy. Neither app's star rating says anything about its accuracy.

Every claim in this report carries one of four evidence labels:

| Label | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | Peer-reviewed studies, official store or regulator records, or facts reported by several major outlets |
| **Moderate** | Rigorous but not yet peer-reviewed work (the NIH abstract), observational studies, journalist hands-on tests, or a failure reported independently by many users |
| **Weak** | Founder or company claims, single reviews, third-party estimates, competitor blogs |
| **Inference** | This report's own reasoning from the evidence |

Three gaps cut across every section. Reddit was not accessed. **Neither app has been independently tested on Indian food.** Neither publishes retention data. Recent Google Play reviews for both apps could not be extracted either, so the user voice here is iPhone-heavy in an Android-heavy country.

## Cal AI sells speed, then loses users when the numbers move

### A teenage side project became MyFitnessPal's speed brand

Cal AI launched on the App Store on 8 April 2024 ([iTunes Lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6480417616&country=us)). Its founders were two 18-year-old high-school students, Zach Yadegari and Henry Langmack, with Blake Anderson, who had built the ChatGPT-based apps RizzGPT and Umax. A COO, Jake Castillo, ran influencer marketing ([TechCrunch 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/16/photo-calorie-app-cal-ai-downloaded-over-a-million-times-was-built-by-two-teenagers/)). MyFitnessPal (MFP) closed its acquisition in December 2025 and announced it in March 2026. MFP credited Cal AI with **15M+ downloads and $30M+ in annual revenue in under two years**; these figures are unaudited (Weak). Cal AI still runs as a separate app, now "integrated with" MFP's database of about 20 million foods, 68,500 brands and 380+ restaurant chains. MFP's CEO described its position bluntly: **"Cal AI is for those preferring speed over accuracy"** ([TechCrunch 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/02/myfitnesspal-has-acquired-cal-ai-the-viral-calorie-app-built-by-teens)). Yadegari left in June 2026 to found a wearables startup ([TechCrunch Oct 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/cal-ais-19-year-old-founder-just-raised-10m-for-his-new-ai-startup/)). Since then the app ships weekly, and every release note reads "Improvements and tweaks to help you build and be your best" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/cal-ai-calorie-tracker/id6480417616)). One reviewer's experience of that cadence: "for the first two or three days after every update, it's like it forgets how to do its job" ([US feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json)).

Growth came from paid creators. Yadegari says Cal AI kept **about 250 influencers on monthly retainers**, chosen for "very tight communities" of at least about 50,000 followers. The first $2,000 post brought about 1,000 downloads and 100 paying users in two days. Paid Facebook spend on creator videos later took monthly revenue from $2M to $4M ([Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/cal-ai-founder-zach-yadegari-breaks-down-the-influencer-playbook-behind-his-apps-growth/); [Founderpath](https://founderpath.com/resources/zach-yadegari-cal-ai-onboarding-tests-and-influencer-marketing)). He also says adding onboarding questions raised paid conversion, and that **20–25% of users start a trial or pay** (Weak, founder claim). The one retention figure, "above 30%" in 2025, has no definition, and TechCrunch couldn't verify it.

### Two or three taps when the first answer is right, delete-and-redo when it isn't

Every log starts from one "+" button. It opens a camera with three modes (Scan Food, Barcode, Food Label), plus a food-database search with a Voice Log button inside it, Manual Add, and saved meals ([ScreensDesign UX flow](https://screensdesign.com/apps/cal-ai-calorie-tracker/?id=1739&ts=0&vt=1)). The result screen shows calories, macros, a health score out of 10, and an ingredient list where each ingredient has its own portion and calories. Below that sit a "Fix Issue" box for corrections in plain words, and "Done". A scan appears to go straight into the day's log: "You also cannot scan a food without adding it to your daily calorie count" ([Play US](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.viraldevelopment.calai&hl=en_US&gl=US)). When the first answer is right, users love it. One says a log "can be done with 2-3 taps MAXIMUM as opposed to spending several minutes when using apps like MyFitnessPal" ([AU feed](https://itunes.apple.com/au/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). No one has independently timed or counted taps for Cal AI.

When the answer is wrong, the fast path falls apart. One reviewer corrected only the calories, from 280 to 250, and "the protein count was correct and it was 10 grams, all of a sudden it will be 7 grams!". Another: "Attempting to use the fix issue button seems to confuse things further and I end up having to delete the entry and start again" ([US feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json); [GB feed](https://itunes.apple.com/gb/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). Portion editing is inconsistent ("sometimes grams, sometimes only a portion"), and changing a portion can reset an entry ([AU feed](https://itunes.apple.com/au/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). A barcode scan of a whole ground-beef package logged one serving. Repeat foods are a sore point despite the listing's "Food Memory" claim: "the same coffee with milk … every morning I have to scroll through to find it. It should be the top two things". Another user photographs "my yogurt pot every day despite the fact it's identical every time", because scanned barcodes never show up in recents. There are no breakfast, lunch or dinner categories, and users ask for them ([CA feed](https://itunes.apple.com/ca/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). Voice logging exists, but it is tucked inside search, and some users don't know it's there ("one is no voice recording"). Cal AI does have home-screen and lock-screen widgets showing "calories left". The widget's barcode button, though, opens the general camera, so the user has to choose barcode a second time. Outside the US the app is slow. A paying Indian user on a Galaxy S26 Ultra reports "3 to 5 minutes just to accurately log a meal" ([Play IN](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.viraldevelopment.calai&hl=en_IN&gl=IN)).

### Nobody outside Cal AI knows where its numbers come from

In 2025 the founders said Cal AI used models from Anthropic and OpenAI with retrieval-augmented generation. That means the app looks up relevant food-database rows and hands them to the model with the question, like passing a lookup table into a function, using open-source food data ([TechCrunch 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/16/photo-calorie-app-cal-ai-downloaded-over-a-million-times-was-built-by-two-teenagers/)). The website claims "your phone's depth sensor calculates food volume" ([calai.app](https://www.calai.app/)). Only iPhone Pro models have the LiDAR depth sensor, and Android has no equivalent route, so for most users portions must be visual guesses by the model (Inference). The privacy policy names no AI providers ([Privacy policy](https://www.calai.app/privacy)). Nothing public says whether a displayed number is a database row, model output or a blend. The way the same food produces different numbers fits model-generated values (Inference). Language models are not repeatable even when their randomness setting, temperature, is 0: one lab sent a prompt 1,000 times at temperature 0 and got **80 different completions** ([Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/)). Yadegari has said that **retention rose in step with accuracy gains on an internal test set** (Weak), which is the best argument that accuracy pays even for a speed-first app ([Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/cal-ai-founder-zach-yadegari-breaks-down-the-influencer-playbook-behind-his-apps-growth/)). In March 2026 a hacker claimed a breach of more than 3 million users' data. The claimed data includes birth dates, weights, PINs and meal logs with eating times, taken from a misconfigured Google Firebase database. It is unverified ([Hackread](https://hackread.com/cal-ai-myfitnesspal-data-breach-3m-users/); [SC World](https://www.scworld.com/brief/over-3m-allegedly-impacted-by-cal-ai-app-breach)).

### Accuracy claims slid from 90% to 80%, and the only lab test found a one-third under-count

Cal AI's own claim has fallen from about 90% in 2025 to **"CalAI is about 80% accurate"** on its November 2026 FAQ, and neither figure comes with a method ([TechCrunch 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/16/photo-calorie-app-cal-ai-downloaded-over-a-million-times-was-built-by-two-teenagers/); [calai.app](https://www.calai.app/)). The best independent evidence is an NIH/NIDDK study. It used 102 meals prepared in a metabolic kitchen, with every ingredient weighed to 0.1 g, and ran standard photos through four apps (Moderate: strong design, but a July 2026 conference abstract that hasn't been peer-reviewed).

| App | Mean energy under-count per meal | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| Appediet | 252 kcal | 210–295 |
| MyFitnessPal | 327 kcal | 269–385 |
| Lose It! | 333 kcal | 282–383 |
| **Cal AI** | **345 kcal** | 296–392 |

That is about a third of each meal. **All four apps missed roughly 30 g of fat**, and high-fat meals were the worst. Researcher Aaron Hengist's advice: people using photo apps "without adjusting the portions or entering the amounts of food should take the results with a grain of salt" ([Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/primary-care/20260804/ai-photobased-calorietracking-tools-underestimate-them-by-33); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/07/260726015237.htm)). A Lifehacker reviewer's hands-on test found misidentification on top of under-counting. A 222 g apple was first read as "tikka masala". A salad of tofu, feta and chickpeas with an oil dressing came out at 450 kcal against her 800–900. Her verdict: checking every entry leaves "the worst of both worlds" ([Lifehacker via Pipedot](https://pipedot.org/6XQ7T)).

Reviews add a failure the lab didn't measure, which is variance. "You can scan the same item 3 different times and get three entirely different percentage". A plate shot from another angle went from 400 to 700 kcal. Oatmeal with bran came out at 480 kcal one day and 870 the next ([US](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json), [GB](https://itunes.apple.com/gb/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json), [AU](https://itunes.apple.com/au/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json) feeds; Moderate, with no controlled repeat test). Users also confirm the NIH fat finding in their own words: "it doesn't know what you cook with so all butter and oil is not accounted for". The errors don't all point one way: "8 medium shrimp" became 35 g of protein. Label reading, which should just be copying numbers, fails too: one shake label became "7250 cal per serve" ([AU feed](https://itunes.apple.com/au/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). Some users now check Cal AI against ChatGPT, which, as one says, "defeats its purpose" ([CA feed](https://itunes.apple.com/ca/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). Two separate problems are at work here. **Bias** is the steady under-count that every photo app shares. **Variance** is different numbers for the same food. Users see variance immediately and read it as "broken"; bias stays hidden until the scale disagrees.

### A 33-screen quiz, a 3-day trial, and rings users fought to keep

Onboarding runs **28 to 33 screens**. They cover gender, workouts, height and weight, goal and goal weight, a weight-loss pace slider, obstacles, diet type, health permissions, "add calories burned back?", "calorie rollover?", the rating prompt, a referral code, a plan-building progress bar and a personalised plan. Sign-up comes last ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/apps/cal-ai-calorie-tracker/?id=1739&ts=0&vt=1)). A soft paywall follows: a 3-day trial that turns into a yearly plan. The listing says "FOOD SCANNING ANALYSIS RESULTS REQUIRE A SUBSCRIPTION". US in-app prices run from $2.99 to $29.99, there is a family plan, and a **$0.99 "Streak Restore"** buys back a broken streak ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/cal-ai-calorie-tracker/id6480417616)). Indian Play prices run from ₹990 to ₹6,500 per item ([Play IN](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.viraldevelopment.calai&hl=en_IN&gl=IN)). In April 2026 Apple briefly removed the app (Strong). Apple said it charged through Stripe without the required in-app purchase, displayed a weekly-equivalent price more prominently than the amount actually billed, used a trial toggle that hid auto-renewal, and pushed users who declined into a second subscription offer ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/04/21/apple-cal-ai-app-store-removal/); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/04/21/popular-calorie-tracker-briefly-pulled-from-app-store-over-iap-and-billing-violations/)).

The home screen leads with remaining calories and rings for calories and macros. Below are a calendar strip, a health score, a streak card and a "recently uploaded" list; fibre and sodium sit behind a swipe. Dark mode was promoted as a new feature. In October 2026 Cal AI recoloured the rings red, yellow and green by health score. Reviews titled "Please get rid of the new ring color system" called red rings demotivating, and the change was reverted ([US feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json); [CA feed](https://itunes.apple.com/ca/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). The health score also contradicts the rings. One user had 1,000 calories to spare and was still told they had exceeded their targets. Another got "4/10 for plain apples". Apple Health sync reads steps but, users say, "doesn't send nutritional information to Apple health" ([AU feed](https://itunes.apple.com/au/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). One user says history "caps out at 3 weeks"; another says "You cannot download your data".

Retention runs on streaks, milestone badges, groups, a $10 referral and heavy notifications, and several of these now earn one-star reviews. Paid streak repair: "I lost my 60 day streak but I can restore it by paying $0.99!". Groups: one was shown as "under maintenance", group names carried explicit language, and a user who took a few weeks off lost "my group and our chat history". Notifications: "around 9 identical 'Hungry? Open Cal AI' notifications back to back… The trial end reminder never came through, I got billed" ([CA](https://itunes.apple.com/ca/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json), [AU](https://itunes.apple.com/au/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json), [US](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json), [GB](https://itunes.apple.com/gb/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json) feeds). Streaks do work for some users ("The streak keeps me motivated to track my calories"). **Cal AI has no coach.** Its AI support bot "repeatedly tells you to follow the same steps that I outlined don't work".

### Users praise the ease and punish the billing, and Indian users say it doesn't know their food

Keyword matching over the 1,007 reviews (approximate, Moderate) puts **billing, trials and refunds first, in 294 reviews, 236 of them one-star**. Next come rings and the health score (132), portions (108), fixes (89), search (55), barcodes (50), and Indian or international food (33). Praise is about ease and results: "Down 76 lbs. Worth every penny." Even critics grant the design: "the only thing it does better than other apps is the UI design" ([US](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json), [AU](https://itunes.apple.com/au/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json) feeds). Goals can be dangerous: one user says the app "told me a 500cal diet was my best", and Lifehacker's tester was given a target that would have made her underweight.

Indian and South Asian users are consistent. "Compared with Healthify, this app doesn't even come close to the food catalogue it hosts. Especially for asian and indian dishes." "They drastically fail to identify Indian meal nutritions." Another has "trouble with Indian foods (although, GPT does it great, so not sure where the problem is)" ([IN feed](https://itunes.apple.com/in/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). In Canada: "Not Suitable for South Asian food" ([CA feed](https://itunes.apple.com/ca/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). UK users have to type "French fries" because the app doesn't know "chips" ([GB feed](https://itunes.apple.com/gb/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). The "GPT does it great" remark suggests the gap is in Cal AI's food data and retrieval, not in the model's raw ability (Inference). The Indian sample is small (71 reviews), and no controlled test of Cal AI on Indian food exists.

## Healthify owns India's food data, then spends its goodwill on sales

### A 2012 spreadsheet became a coaching business with 45 million sign-ups

Tushar Vashisht, Mathew Cherian and Sachin Shenoy founded HealthifyMe in Bangalore in January 2012. It began as an Excel sheet of Indian foods and calories. The AI nutritionist Ria followed in 2017, and "Snap" photo logging in 2019 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HealthifyMe)). It has raised about $125M in equity and claimed "40 million+ users, 300+ cities, 600+ coaches" in October 2024 ([LeapFrog 2024](https://leapfroginvest.com/press-release/healthify-raises-20m-in-fresh-capital-to-drive-us-expansion/)). Its Indian filings show the business model plainly. In FY23 it earned **₹228.76 Cr, of which ₹139.24 Cr (about 61%) came from coaching**. It spent **₹115.90 Cr on advertising, roughly half its revenue**, and lost about ₹142 Cr ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/healthifyme-highest-ever-revenue-fy23-losses-fall/); Strong, from filings). In December 2025 it reported **"more than 45 million registered users and a few million monthly active users"** ([TechCrunch 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/02/healthify-upgrades-its-ai-assistant-ria-with-real-time-conversation-capabilities)). That implies well under 10% of sign-ups use it in a given month. Tens of millions of Indians have tried a calorie tracker and left, and STEADY can reach them (Inference). Management's attention has moved abroad. Healthify launched a $20-a-month AI plan in the US, then in August 2026 merged with Berry Street, a US clinical provider with 2,000+ clinicians that prescribes GLP-1 weight-loss drugs. It calls its GLP-1 programme its "fastest-growing offering" ([LeapFrog 2026](https://leapfroginvest.com/press-release/healthify-and-berry-street-merge-to-create-advance-ai-metabolic-health-platform/)). Store ratings stay high: 4.55 from 66,646 ratings on the Indian App Store, and 4.43 from 568,464 on Play with 10M+ downloads ([iTunes Lookup IN](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=943712366&country=in); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.healthifyme.basic&hl=en_IN&gl=IN)).

### The food database is both the moat and the credibility problem

The founders built the original list with India's National Institute of Nutrition. From 2012 it offered "measurements in cups, Katori's and bowls", and users could adjust a dish's raw ingredients ([YourStory, via search snippet](https://yourstory.com/2012/12/healthifyme-an-indian-nutrition-tracker-from-the-people-behind-the-rs100-a-day-experiment)). Healthify still calls it "the world's first and largest Indian food database". Its size claims don't agree with each other. Play says "over 100,000 foods". In 2023 the company said it recognised "over one million global foods". The 2026 App Store listing says the database is "not 1 Million or 10 Million, it's INFINITE" ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.healthifyme.basic&hl=en_IN&gl=IN); [AIM](https://analyticsindiamag.com/how-this-startup-is-making-you-eat-healthy-with-generative-ai/); [iTunes Lookup IN](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=943712366&country=in)). Going from 100,000 to "infinite" most plausibly means AI estimates now fill in where curated rows run out (Inference). If so, the same food can come out differently depending on whether the user searched, snapped or described it.

The 2026 reviews describe exactly the failures STEADY is trying to design out (Moderate). "Every food item I search has multiple (sometimes over 10 similar listings) listings with similar names in both Hindi and English, and mostly different calories … No tech to map synonyms … Black Chick Peas boiled is 161 calories for 100g but same amount of Boiled Kala Chana Chaat is just 97." A chaat contains onion, tomato and lemon, so some of that gap may be real. The actual failure is that nothing links the names, so the user can't tell which row to trust. A paying subscriber says packaged food "does not reflect the nutients as per the label … there is no option to adjust manually". Others flag Country Delight protein milk, low-fat paneer and green moong dal, and one says "It thinks air has protein." Foods users suggest never get added ([App Store IN p1](https://itunes.apple.com/in/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=943712366/sortBy=mostRecent/json); [p2](https://itunes.apple.com/in/rss/customerreviews/page=2/id=943712366/sortBy=mostRecent/json); [p3](https://itunes.apple.com/in/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=943712366/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). The same reviews concede its strength: "Macro calculations are generally quite accurate for items that already exist in the database", and the app "covers almost all Our Indian dishes".

### Every input mode exists, but the core path is slow and uneditable

Healthify offers more input modes than anyone else. There is text search with Indian units, and Snap, which works from the camera or the gallery. "Auto Snap" watches the photo gallery and logs meals "without having to open the app". There is also a barcode scanner, Ria chat, and, since December 2025, live voice that accepts several meals in one go in **50+ languages, including 14 Indian languages and mixed Hinglish**. Voice also works through WhatsApp and Ray-Ban Meta glasses ([iTunes Lookup IN](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=943712366&country=in); [TechCrunch 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/02/healthify-upgrades-its-ai-assistant-ria-with-real-time-conversation-capabilities)). The voice coach is built on **OpenAI's Realtime API** ([CXO Digital Pulse](https://www.cxodigitalpulse.com/healthify-launches-ria-voice-a-real-time-multimodal-ai-coach-powered-by-openais-realtime-api/)). The company now claims "patented food logging that tracks nutrition from a photo in under one second", with no accuracy figure attached (Weak).

Long-time users describe a much slower app. A five-year user: "Takes hours to load … only keeps promoting plans. Takes 5-10 mins to enter and track a meal." Another: "the meal tracker takes another minute or two to load". Every meal gets labelled "morning snacks". And: "Please stop with all the pop ups!!! Just show me a simple damn Home Screen and one click to take me to track my calories!!!!" ([App Store IN p1](https://itunes.apple.com/in/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=943712366/sortBy=mostRecent/json); [p3](https://itunes.apple.com/in/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=943712366/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). AI results can't be fixed: "you not able to correct name or macros if it is identified wrongly … when you take a snap again the ai picks the same wrong one." Custom meals hang, and backdated food lands on the wrong date. Healthify **turned off Apple Health write-back while still reading from it**, and at least one user left over it: "removing it caused me to move back to other Apps." Users also report no data export, no iOS widget and no Watch app. Some describe permission dark patterns, forced meal-plan setup, and nudges toward vegetarian food that read as ideology: "Don't disguise a dietary preference as a health recommendation" ([App Store IN p1](https://itunes.apple.com/in/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=943712366/sortBy=mostRecent/json); [p2](https://itunes.apple.com/in/rss/customerreviews/page=2/id=943712366/sortBy=mostRecent/json)).

### Ria runs mostly on OpenAI; the moat is coaching conversations, not models

Ria became a generative, multimodal coach in December 2023. At that point it drew on several model providers (AWS, Meta, OpenAI and open source) and a dataset of "over 350 million messages and diet plans" ([AIM](https://analyticsindiamag.com/how-this-startup-is-making-you-eat-healthy-with-generative-ai/)). By December 2025 the company said it "largely uses OpenAI models". Ria can now read fitness, sleep and glucose data, and a "persistent memory layer" is still only planned ([TechCrunch 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/02/healthify-upgrades-its-ai-assistant-ria-with-real-time-conversation-capabilities)). Healthify's AI advantage is therefore proprietary data, not a better model. STEADY, using GPT-4o and Claude, starts at no structural model disadvantage (Inference).

### Snap claims 60–75% recognition, and its one independent test had no Indian dishes

When Healthify relaunched Snap with automatic image recognition in September 2023, it claimed **60–70% recognition accuracy** from a model trained on 150,000 Indian food items. Human reviewers examined wrong recognitions, and the company named thali portion sizes as a challenge ([TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/09/21/khosla-backed-healthifyme-introduces-ai-powered-image-recognition-for-indian-food/)). By November 2023 the claim was 75% ([Business Today](https://businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/shazam-for-food-healthifymes-snap-ai-tool-makes-tracking-calories-fun-and-easy-404784-2023-11-08); Weak). The only independent test is Li et al. (Strong but narrow). University of Sydney dietitians, publishing in *Nutrients* in 2024, put 22 standardised photos through several leading apps. **HealthifyMe identified 35 of 39 food components (90%)**, behind MyFitnessPal's 97%. Its single-item energy estimates were off by a mean of +8%, the smallest error of the four apps that estimate energy automatically. Individual items still swung widely: **boiled egg +37%, latte +74%**, and in eggs on toast the fried egg was +75% while the butter was −26%. **No Indian dishes were tested**; the "Asian" set was stir fry, pho, bibimbap and pearl milk tea ([Li et al.](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11314244/fullTextXML)). So Healthify mostly recognises food correctly and goes wrong on portions and recipes. A user's test makes the same point: "I have two bowls of different sizes with same food … it shows almost same calories in both bowls. But the difference in bowl size is 1:5." In 2026 Snap was also reported broken for weeks at a time ([App Store IN p2](https://itunes.apple.com/in/rss/customerreviews/page=2/id=943712366/sortBy=mostRecent/json)).

### Coaching works when a person shows up and sours when they sell

The strongest evidence for coaching comes from Healthify's own users. Stanford, IIM and Michigan researchers matched about **65,000 HealthifyMe users over three years**. Over three months, **people with AI plus a human coach lost about 2.7% of body weight, against 1.5% for AI alone**, and the human-coached group "logged their weight and food intake more often" ([Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/ai-can-coach-you-lose-weight-human-touch-still-helps)). This is Moderate evidence: the study is observational, and users who chose human coaches also set higher goals. Healthify's 2026 claim of a peer-reviewed "AI-only" outcomes paper with Stanford Medical School could not be verified.

Coaching is also where the sales funnel lives. In 2023 the basic AI plan cost ₹399 a month and Pro with human coaches started at about ₹4,000 a month, with CGM and diagnostics bundles above that ([TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/09/21/khosla-backed-healthifyme-introduces-ai-powered-image-recognition-for-indian-food/)). In 2026 reviews, users mention paying ₹1,000 for an annual Healthify+ plan, ₹1,499 or ₹1,800 for other subscriptions, and figures as high as ₹15,000. Their experience: "I use to get 15 to 20 calls each day after installing this app"; "Free consultation with dietitian is just for another paid upgradation". A coach "sent me a link to pay the full amount" without asking. A salesperson's verbal promise of a free smart scale was later withdrawn. "Every 2 months coaches will leave … there will be no real handover." A diet plan stayed "exactly the same for the whole 4 months", and one user concluded: "If i just wanted a plan, i would use AI instead" ([App Store IN p1](https://itunes.apple.com/in/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=943712366/sortBy=mostRecent/json); [p3](https://itunes.apple.com/in/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=943712366/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). How often the calls come rests on single reviews (Weak). That sales pressure is common is Moderate evidence. When coaching works, users credit named coaches with 8–40 kg lost and normal HbA1c (long-term blood sugar) readings again. Play's most-helpful review, with 477 votes, complains that the trial-and-nutritionist pop-up appears "every time I open the app" ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.healthifyme.basic&hl=en_IN&gl=IN)).

Healthify's engagement features are standard: daily challenges, a leaderboard, community, and daily and weekly reports. ## One list: borrow, avoid, win

Each item below names who it comes from, how strong the evidence is, and where it lands in STEADY's plan. **Pipeline** means the accuracy rebuild underneath the redesign. It has five parts. First, a ranked search that always returns the same row for the same words, with an alias table that maps roti, chapati and phulka to one entry. Second, portion weights stored as data, so the model picks a unit like "1 katori" and code looks up the grams. Third, per-user memory of confirmed foods and portions. Fourth, a validation gate that rejects impossible rows; for example, the Atwater check recomputes calories as 4 × protein + 4 × carbs + 9 × fat, and no food can top about 900 kcal per 100 g ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/3/Y5022E/y5022e04.htm)). Fifth, an upgraded eval that measures signed bias, meaning whether errors lean high or low, and whether repeat runs give identical results. The release stages are the founder's: **v2.0** (Home, rings and logging), **v2.1** (Journal and evening check-in) and **v2.2** (widgets). **Later** means monetisation and new markets. **Policy** means rules that apply in every release.

### Over-counting is the gainer's worst error, so the accuracy rebuild comes first

Every independent test of the competitors finds under-counting. STEADY's own users find the opposite: numbers that run high. For a person trying to lose weight, under-counting is the dangerous direction. For a lean gainer, over-counting is. Take a gainer with a 3,000 kcal target whose meals are over-counted by 15%. On a day the ring reports 3,000, they actually ate about 2,600, which may be maintenance for them. The gainer-zone ring would celebrate a surplus that never happened, and the scale would stay flat for weeks before anyone noticed. Competitors' under-counting would accidentally push gainers into *bigger* surpluses. STEADY's current error does the reverse and quietly defeats its own lead persona (Inference, resting on the NIH result and the founder's observation of STEADY's numbers). There is a second trap. Photos in STEADY probably inherit the industry's under-count, while text and voice run high, so the day's total could mix errors of opposite sign. The eval therefore has to report signed bias **separately for each input mode**. Ship the gainer zone only once day-level bias sits inside a target, for example ±5% (this report's proposal). This doesn't change the decision; it changes the order. The data fixes go before the ring, because the ring is only as honest as the number feeding it.

### Photos should be an equal way in, not an equal source of grams

The evidence backs photo as an entry point and undercuts photo as a measuring tool. In the Sydney study, Healthify identified 90% of foods but missed energy by up to 74% on single items, because portions and recipes were wrong ([Li et al.](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11314244/fullTextXML)). The NIH team's own advice is to adjust portions instead of trusting the photo ([Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/primary-care/20260804/ai-photobased-calorietracking-tools-underestimate-them-by-33)). A Healthify user's two bowls, five times apart in size, came out at almost the same calories. Cal AI's "depth sensor" exists only on iPhone Pro models. So keep photo as an equal *way in*. Let it identify foods and read labels, and let grams come from the user's learned portions, chips or scale mode. Photo-based sizing should be a visible starting guess, never the final grams for a mixed plate. Photo results should auto-log only when the user already has a learned portion for that food. This is Moderate evidence, and it also answers the open question of how central photo logging should be.

### Groups carry costs the redesign does not need yet

The founder kept groups. Cal AI's groups have gone "under maintenance", carried explicit group names, and lost users' chat histories after a break ([AU feed](https://itunes.apple.com/au/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json); [US feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). Groups bring moderation, privacy (other people seeing your meals) and more notifications. STEADY's own status tracker already lists an open red bug in which a group push leaves a user with no group on a spinner with no back button. It also lists a group nudge feature with nothing in the app that calls it (`STATUS.md`). With 36 users, the evidence (Weak) argues for keeping groups working as they are behind the old interface, leaving them out of v2.0–v2.2, and redesigning them only once usage data justifies it.

### Health write-back and export belong in the release plan

None of the three rivals writes nutrition back to Apple Health or Health Connect, which are the phone's shared health stores that other apps read from. Healthify switched it off, and users said in writing that they left because of it ([App Store IN](https://itunes.apple.com/in/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=943712366/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). Journable has called it "coming soon" since March 2025 ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/journable-ai-calorie-counter/id6477719720?see-all=reviews)). Neither feature appears in the founder's phased plan. Both need native code that Expo Go can't load. Expo Go is a prebuilt app that runs only JavaScript plus the native modules bundled into it. The founder has already decided to move to development builds, which are STEADY's own compiled app, at the start of the redesign, so this becomes possible at little extra cost. Put nutrition write-back (HealthKit on iOS, Health Connect on Android), CSV export and full history into v2.1 alongside the Journal (Moderate).

### Meals need labels the user never has to pick

Here the evidence pulls two ways. Cal AI users ask, "Why wouldn't it let you categorize your food by breakfast, lunch and dinner", and want items grouped into meals ([CA feed](https://itunes.apple.com/ca/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). Yet choosing a meal slot adds a decision to every log, and real meal times drift by 1.5–2 hours from day to day ([Tran et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12340925/)). Healthify shows what happens when automatic labels go wrong: "every meal is incorrectly labeled as 'morning snacks'" ([App Store IN p3](https://itunes.apple.com/in/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=943712366/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). The answer that fits the Journal decision is to label meals automatically, from the time of day and the user's own pattern. Never ask, allow one tap to change it, and group items logged in one sentence or within a short window into a single meal card (Weak to Moderate).

### Lead post-India markets with the diaspora, and price the AI budget for Indian wallets

In the US and Canada, Cal AI now has MFP's 68,500 brands and 380+ restaurant chains behind it ([TechCrunch 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/02/myfitnesspal-has-acquired-cal-ai-the-viral-calorie-app-built-by-teens)). A small team cannot match that branded and restaurant depth head-on. The same reviews, though, show South Asian users in Canada saying Cal AI is "Not Suitable for South Asian food", and UK users fighting over the word "chips" ([CA feed](https://itunes.apple.com/ca/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json); [GB feed](https://itunes.apple.com/gb/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). The order of markets can stay. The way in should change: enter each new market through its South Asian diaspora, where STEADY's Indian data is the advantage, and add regional vocabulary before general marketing. The evidence is a handful of reviews (Weak), and nothing was collected on the Gulf. Healthify once planned a Middle East launch, and no source confirms it happened ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HealthifyMe)).

The AI budget also needs an Indian price check. Users report paying **₹1,000 for an annual Healthify+ plan**, and ₹1,499 to ₹1,800 for other subscriptions ([App Store IN](https://itunes.apple.com/in/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=943712366/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). One reviewer converted a Rs 1,999 Cal AI purchase as "~$25" ([Cal AI IN feed](https://itunes.apple.com/in/rss/customerreviews/id=6480417616/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). At that rate of about ₹80 to the dollar, and assuming these are yearly plans, Indian AI-tracker subscriptions bring in roughly **$1–2 a month per payer, before store fees and tax**. So a $3-a-month AI cost would exceed what a typical Indian subscriber pays, and most users won't pay at all. The logging path itself is cheap. At current list prices, parsing costs about $0.04–0.54 per user per month, and transcription about $0.15–0.85 ([OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)). The open variable is the evening voice check-in and the coach. Healthify built its voice coach on OpenAI's real-time speech model, and this research did not price real-time voice. Keep $1–3 as the ceiling before revenue, but aim for about $1 per active Indian user, and price the check-in before choosing a speech-to-speech model. That rests on Moderate evidence for the prices plus Inference.

## What nobody has measured yet

The published evidence runs out exactly where STEADY's bets are. Several gaps are cheap for STEADY to close itself.

| Gap | Why it matters | How STEADY can close it |
|---|---|---|
| Reddit (r/indianfitness and similar) not accessed | Indian community opinion of both apps is missing, and the review sample leans iPhone | Read by hand; ask the 36 current users which trackers they've tried and why they left |
| **No independent test of Cal AI or Healthify on Indian food** | "Better on Indian food" is STEADY's central claim and nobody has measured it | Run STEADY's held-out Indian set through Cal AI (3-day trial), Healthify (free tier) and Journable; measure signed bias and whether five repeat runs agree |
| **No retention data for either app** | No benchmark for STEADY's 7-day and 30-day retention; Cal AI's "above 30%" is undefined | Track STEADY's own retention by sign-up week in PostHog; compare against itself before and after the redesign |

| Healthify's gram weights for a katori, a roti, a plate | Can't compare STEADY's portion defaults against the incumbent | Inspect inside the app; weigh 20–30 home portions |
| Both apps' pipelines undisclosed | Database versus model numbers is inferred, not confirmed | Photograph and describe the same meal five times in each app |
| Real-time voice cost | Decides whether the check-in fits the budget | Price the candidate speech models before building v2.1 |

## Conclusion

Both incumbents have told the market what they value. MyFitnessPal positions Cal AI as "speed over accuracy". Healthify's revenue comes from phone-sold coaching, and its attention has moved to US clinical care and GLP-1 drugs. In India, the lane of an accurate, calm, Indian-first tracker is empty because neither business model pays for it, not because nobody thought of it. That makes STEADY's lack of monetisation in v1 an asset as well as a constraint. STEADY can promise no calls, no pop-ups, a free barcode scanner and a streak that is never sold. Each of those promises would cost an incumbent real revenue to match. The promises only hold if they are made public before STEADY starts charging.

The deeper lesson is about which errors users punish. Across all three apps, the visible failure, numbers that change for the same meal, causes the anger. The invisible one, steady bias, does the real damage, and for a gainer it shows up weeks later as a flat scale. That sets STEADY's order: make numbers repeatable first, because users can see it; then fix the direction of the bias, because gainers feel it; then widen coverage, because that decides who switches. The advantages that last are the ones a rival can't buy by changing model provider. Healthify's 350 million coaching messages and MFP's 20 million foods are out of reach. A clean Indian alias table, weighed home portions and each user's learned servings are within reach, and they get better with every log.
